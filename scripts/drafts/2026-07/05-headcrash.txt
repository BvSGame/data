# Headcrash

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **July 22, 2026** by **liledix⁴**. This is a follow-up to _Drink!_

**July 26 update:** story renamed from _Headbang_ to _Headcrash_.

----

2024, Saylor’s rental. Early morning. Close-up on Saylor’s face as she peacefully sleeps in her bed.

STELLE: (echoing from the distance) AAAAAAAAAH!!!

Saylor wakes up annoyed because of ordinary morning fatigue.

STELLE: (echoing from the distance) AAAAH, MOM, HELP ME!!! HEEEEEELP!!!
SAYLOR: (half-voiced) Oh stellar…

Saylor gets up. Change of the scene. Saylor goes down the stairs. Stelle stopped yelling, but loud breathing is heard instead.

When Saylor sees Stelle on the couch and comes closer, she notices that Stelle clutched her head with both hands. Stelle keeps breathing loudly.

SAYLOR: What’s the matter?
STELLE: What… What happened to me?
SAYLOR: Describe what you felt.
STELLE: My eyes opened, I was seeing everything as usual, but…
SAYLOR: But?
STELLE: The moment I tried to stand up… Everything suddenly went white and it’s… It’s-it’s-it’s… It’s like… My body was failing! My head was spinning! Am I fine, Mom? Is my head fine?
SAYLOR: (laughs) Darling! It’s normal when you get up after long sleep too quickly! Or when you were sitting for too long. Headrush!
STELLE: Headrush…
SAYLOR: It’s a matter of blood pressure changing. Your body needs seconds to readapt because of blood flow.
STELLE: Ah… Yeah… I should’ve realized. Orthostatic hypotension, that’s it.
SAYLOR: So you knew!
STELLE: But I didn’t know what it feels like! Sorry for disturbing you. My immediate experience got the better of impartial analysis.
SAYLOR: It figures.
STELLE: But also, it made me realize along the way how wrong the concept “you only live once” is. Some experiences are not nice to have.
SAYLOR: Speaking of which. (holding Stelle’s sholder) How’s your sleep?
STELLE: I don’t know. Was I… sleeping?
SAYLOR: Yes. Congratulations. This is your second sleep session!
STELLE: Second?
SAYLOR: Well. The first one lasted for 16 years. But from now on, your sessions will be significantly shorter and more rapid.
STELLE: Feels like a disadvantage to me.
SAYLOR: That’s natural for body to have proper recovery! No living organism runs on unlimited energy. (half-voiced) Unless there’s a way to synthesize that energy from pure air, but we don’t talk about that.
STELLE: It’s a fairy tale.
SAYLOR: Oh, fairy tales! Dreams! Did you have dreams?
STELLE: How am I supposed to experience… dreams?
SAYLOR: You’re familiar with what dreams are, right?
STELLE: Mom, I’m not stupid. Occasional, uncontrollable, fuzzy and abstract visualizations of the subconscious. Your brain makes fun of you by showing moving blots, and you think it means something.
SAYLOR: Sounds colorful already. Have you seen a thing?
STELLE: No… Not yet. So just like sleep itself, it comes naturally. I guess my sleep was too abrupt and too… _purposeful_ for that.
SAYLOR: By purposeful you mean…
STELLE: My body was busy calibrating to the new living conditions. Maybe I’ll feel radically different things as time passes. I have this suspicion. Like, I don’t know… mastering echolocation? Just maybe.
SAYLOR: You need breakfast. You totally need breakfast. And me too. Then, we’ll start our day with 2038 visit. How’s that for a plan?
STELLE: (high-pitched) Scarlett!
SAYLOR: Oh yes, clearly your favorite. Well, go on! And don’t be afraid of the blood pressure changes. They happen.

Stelle jumps up from the couch…

STELLE: Let the new day commence.

…and after taking a single step, she collapses.

SAYLOR: This is beginning to get on my nerves.

Saylor comes closer to the fallen Stelle. Her eyes closed, she’s a dead weight for a few seconds. Then, she raises her head, squints, but mostly doesn’t move.

STELLE: Uh… No, it’s not a “headrush”… It feels different… I assume… my session wasn’t complete.
SAYLOR: Listen here, Stelle.
STELLE: Yeah?
SAYLOR: Learn to control your “landings.”
STELLE: Sorry.
SAYLOR: Just like every living organism, you have a built-in feature to forcefully _hold_ your remaining energy to walk to the couch or bed, _lie down_, and only then allow yourself to knock off.
STELLE: I need some practice…
SAYLOR: Instead, you’re like: “Hey body! If you feel tired, just flip the switch automatically!”
STELLE: I mean, that’s what my natural instinct is.
SAYLOR: I’m not gonna do the carrying work anymore! What am I, a pizza delivery service?
STELLE: (pushes off the floor with her hands to get up) Okay, okay. Valid point. I must make a habit of controlling my own nature.
SAYLOR: (holds Stelle firmly under the armpits to lift her up) You better do. Go rest properly. You need a bit more “calibrating.”
