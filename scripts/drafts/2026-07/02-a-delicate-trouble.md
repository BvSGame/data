# A Delicate Trouble

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **July 18, 2026** by **liledix⁴**. This is a follow-up to _Newborn_.

----

2024, Saylor’s rental house. Saylor and Stelle are standing in front of a mirror. Saylor fixes Stelle’s hair carefully.

SAYLOR: So… Your pick is cottagecore, then.
STELLE: Oh, totally. But with an asterisk.
SAYLOR: An asterisk?
STELLE: Yup. Shorter skirt. Somewhere above the knees. Always consider the aerodynamics, especially if you’re a one-day old teenager beaming with energy.
SAYLOR: Looks like you’re living your best life.
STELLE: I’m immensely satisfied.
SAYLOR: Stelle, can I ask something… uh, “uncareful?”
STELLE: Mom, why are you worried? Ask away! I’m listening to everything you say anyway.
SAYLOR: Okay… Remember you were perfectly comfortable having just a sundress? You seemingly were fine with injuring your feet.
STELLE: I’m still fine with that outfit.
SAYLOR: That’s what worries me. I know you didn’t have a lived experience. That’s why I have to watch your every step here and there. Do you… ahem, how to put it… cover sensitive area?
STELLE: You mean…
SAYLOR: You understand what I mean.
STELLE: Mom. I’m not a fool. Of course it’s… been acknowledged.
SAYLOR: “Acknowledging” is a very flexible term, my dear.
STELLE: Okay, okay. I understand your point. It’s not comfortable.
SAYLOR: Only because you’ve spent your entire life wearing nothing at all, that’s why. I’m sorry for being restrictive, but I’ll control everything.
STELLE: Mom!
SAYLOR: For your own safety! Please don’t argue with me. You know I’m right in this argument.
STELLE: Yeah, you’re right… I just… I just can’t see it.
SAYLOR: Can’t see what?
STELLE: What is considered appropriate.
SAYLOR: Sigh… (wrapping her arm around Stelle’s shoulder) Just stand with me and you’ll start seeing. Alright?
STELLE: Okay.
SAYLOR: Just don’t rush in your independence quest. You may make things extremely worse. I don’t want to fix problems after you.
STELLE: I’m sorry for being a trouble, Mom.
SAYLOR: (pulling Stelle closer) Don’t worry. You’re _my_ trouble.

They both have a laugh.

SAYLOR: But underwear? Never forget! That is an order.
STELLE: Mom—
SAYLOR: Do not force me to write a thesis and make you read thrice. Just to prove my point. Because I can absolutely do it.
STELLE: Mom, please—
SAYLOR: Twenty thousand words long.
STELLE: You win.
SAYLOR: That’s my girl.

Suddenly, Stelle’s smartwatch began vibrating.

STELLE: Oh hell.
SAYLOR: Vocabulary approved.
STELLE: Thank you. Well well well, we have 2038 friends worrying about us… or me.
SAYLOR: It’s your watch. Answer it.

Stelle answers the call.

TONI: Hello?
STELLE: Toni? The 1860 girl?
TONI: Totally me! Where are you bunch of bigheads stuck?
STELLE: The “bighead” term is derogatory, you know that, right?
TONI: Oh don’t be dull. I’m, well, making my vocabulary colorful. Perhaps we have something in common.
STELLE: Okay, never mind that, why are you calling?
TONI: Come on, kid. We miss you. And it’s not like we’re being sentimental here, but we miss you tactically! Grab your car and move your butts right here!
STELLE: Shush, Mom’s listening.
SAYLOR: I don’t mind, though.
SCARLETT: Uh, Toni, go for a walk, please, I’ll take the phone now.
TONI: Please yourself, rogue super-agent.
SCARLETT: Rude. Ahem. Stelle?
SAYLOR: Hello, I’m here too, Scarlett!
SCARLETT: Look, Saylor. If I needed you, I would call you.
SAYLOR: Oh it’s Stelle Day today then, okay-okay.
STELLE: Mom, don’t be smug.
SAYLOR: Sorry, girl. I’ll… go check the SUV.
SCARLETT: Stelle, how are you doing?
STELLE: I’m good. I don’t recognize your tone. Why so soft? You’re usually dead serious.
SCARLETT: Let’s face it. You aren’t trained enough for me to be pressing on you. Fair, right?
STELLE: I can see your logic. But I’m not incompetent.
SCARLETT: I don’t doubt your competence. I question your experience. You’re still in the process of gaining it. But don’t count it as a disadvantage. Meet me when I’m off-duty. Then we’ll talk if Saylor is enough of the motherly figure for you.
SAYLOR: I’ve heard that!
STELLE: Shut up and focus on the objective!
SCARLETT: That’s my girl. You’re learning really quickly. I’m so looking forward to your progress.
SAYLOR: You know, Stelle, sometimes you confuse the right people worth listening to.
STELLE: Don’t get pleased with yourself, Mom.
SCARLETT: Keep this spirit. We may need it. Darkrock may not stand a chance against these opening opportunities. See you later. 14 years later, if you will.
STELLE: Yeah, time travel is a headache.
SCARLETT: Everyone at the agency said that. I’m already used to it.
LISA: Is Stelle coming or isn’t she?
SCARLETT: Lisa, learn to have patience.
LISA: You don’t teach me patience.
SCARLETT: And I’m running low on it. Stelle, I’ll be waiting. Now there’s a work to do. Ta-ta for now.
LISA: Tough. Stelle, I’ve already downloaded myself to your smartwatch. If you ever need me physically, you can ping me and I’ll assist you with a map and detailed directions. My geolocation skills are unmatched.
STELLE: For a robot, that’s incredibly impressive.
LISA: Nuh-uh. Wrong term. I’m not a robot. I’m Human Plus.
STELLE: Yeah, and I’m the President of Earth.
LISA: Bad joke. Because in reality, it’s another person.
STELLE: I know.
LISA: May I ask how exactly?
STELLE: There’s a person who never shuts up about time travel.
SAYLOR: That is a part of the mandatory learning process. See? I’m not useless.
LISA: (utilizes both Stelle’s and Saylor’s smartwatches) Saylor, SUV. Quickly.
SAYLOR: I’m doing my best! Don’t push me!
LISA: (back to just Stelle’s smartwatch) Stelle, I expect you to learn the time machine mechanics quicker. Saylor may be… unreliable. I’ll feed you with information.
STELLE: I can only read it in a digital form. My pod days are in the past.
LISA: I don’t ask more.
