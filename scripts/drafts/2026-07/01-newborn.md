# Newborn

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **July 18, 2026** by **liledix⁴**.

----

The 14th millennium. Saylor walks in some dark room that is barely lit. She notices some pod. A small window shows there’s a young female person contained in that pod, submerged in some liquid. The eyes are closed, the person doesn’t react. No consciousness.

Saylor taps the window casually. Nothing. No reaction. Saylor notices, there’s a little computer attached to the pod. Saylor operates it a little. At some point, she presses the screen with her palm. A few seconds of pause, Saylor furrowed her brow, then the liquid began to bubble and fizz vigorously, but the girl in the container showed no reaction. Her hair fluttered, her body shifted slightly, but she showed no signs of being conscious. Saylor instantly removed her hand from the pod and took a few steps back.

SAYLOR: Uh… Uh-oh. Please tell me this isn’t a sci-fi blender. I ate recently, don’t make me uncomfy.

Fizzing stopped. Pause. The liquid started slowly draining out.

SAYLOR: (taps the glass again) Uh, wanna ask nicely, is there an option to return that liquid back? Thank you very much.

Nobody responds, nothing responds. The girl slowly blinks and opens her eyes. She gasps for air so loudly and abruptly that Saylor shuddered for a brief moment.

SAYLOR: Uh, hi. Hey, sorry, I mean you no harm. Well, unless this liquid was serving as a life support. In which case, oops, I killed you, gotta dash before I get killed earlier. Or later. I don’t like getting killed. Not my hobby.

The girl doesn’t react, she only looks around through the window, scanning the room and taking a good look at Saylor.

SAYLOR: Hey! Don’t look at me like I broke the law! File a complaint to your manufacturers! I’m only a humble user of the… system I don’t understand, and I demand an end-user license agreement! I know you have a voice interaction. Do you hear me?

The draining stopped. Silence.

SAYLOR: (closes her eyes) Okay. This is it. Your chance to kill me. Go on. Turn me into a puddle before I do it myself.
GIRL: Mother?
SAYLOR: No. I’m… (opens her eyes) What? Uh. (slowly, backing away, almost melodically) A-a-awkwa-a-ard… Gotta go. Not nice to meet you. Bye! See you never!
GIRL: Stay here. You’re my mother.
SAYLOR: (stopped, regained confidence, arms akimbo) I’m a mother of no one. Do not imply that I’m retiring from my adventures and go babysitting. It insults my intelligence.
GIRL: You are the one who touched the scanner.
SAYLOR: Yes. A casual physical input interface. I’ve seen better in some past centuries. It’s too laggy, it doesn’t meet my performance standards. Now if you’ll excuse me, whoever you are, I’m off.
GIRL: I kindly asked to stay here, Mom.
SAYLOR: Do not call me—

The door opens. The girl steps out. However, there’s a nuance. In the shot, we see only her silhouette from behind, in close-up.

SAYLOR: (squeaky, covers her eyes) Ah!!
GIRL: What’s the matter? Why have you covered your eyes?
SAYLOR: Well. I should’ve seen this coming. But… you’re naked.
GIRL: Naturally. I can’t be born with clothes. They don’t materialize during biological processes in a confined space.
SAYLOR: (eyes still covered but she walks anyway) Okay. Stand right here. Don’t go anywhere, don’t follow me. I need to go and fetch something for you to… uh, cover up.
GIRL: I see no issues. I’m perfectly equipped.
SAYLOR: No, you literally have absolutely nothing!

Saylor rushes to the locker room nearby.

GIRL: (to herself) Uh. Head… Nose, ears, hair… Oh, long. Brown? I wanted green… Two hands… Uh, two legs… Fingers, toes… Twenty in total, good. All functional. Oh, good, I have a belly… (louder) Mom! I told you! I’m fully equipped! I’ve checked myself and I have every necessary component of my body!

Then, a girl silhouette walks to Saylor.

SAYLOR: (from the locker room) Uh-huh. Except for a navel. Where’s your navel?
GIRL: I don’t need one.
SAYLOR: Then you’re not born. You’re created. There’s a difference. Therefore, I’m not your mother. Stop calling me like that.
GIRL: You’ve interacted with my interface.
SAYLOR: It doesn’t mean anything! I interact with interfaces on a daily basis! Uh… Okay, it sounds horrible. Forget what I said. There’s one thing you definitely forgot. I told you explicitly not to follow me.
GIRL: I needed to make sure you don’t have any hostile intent.

Saylor turns to the girl, holding out a purple sundress. This time, she ignores her being naked.

SAYLOR: If hostile intent means covering you up with this dress, then I do have some. Put this on and stop walking around like it’s a public bathhouse.
GIRL: This is not green.
SAYLOR: Put it on before I decide a fashionable jumpsuit fits you better. Which is… not a bad idea. (to herself) Why have I framed it like it’s something bad?

The girl takes the sundress and proceeds.

GIRL: I have a critically important question. Who am I?
SAYLOR: Wait. You don’t know? Ah, of course… The baby thing. You’re playing a toddler, right? Well-articulate grown-up toddler. Or it’s some sort of heavy amnesia you have. May I ask how old are you truly?

We finally see the girl properly from the front. She’s smoothing out her dress and finishing fixing its shoulder straps. She’s fixing her hair that is wavy and still wet. She’s slightly shorter than Saylor.

GIRL: Well, let’s figure out, shall we?

They walk back to the pod the girl just left. She interacts with the interface to check the metrics.

GIRL: Uh-huh. Readings claim that I’m 16 years old. But who am I?
SAYLOR: What do you mean? Name?
GIRL: Yes.
SAYLOR: How do I know? Figure it out for yourself. I guess in your times you’ve long ditched the social construct of parents giving names to their children. Don’t you have an ID card?
GIRL: You’re my mother and it’s you who should make a decision.
SAYLOR: May I not? I’m just Saylor, I don’t make decisions to name children I don’t have.
GIRL: The scanner analyzed the DNA from your hand and synthesized a lineage sample of it for my body in a flash. Result: boom, I’m your daughter.
SAYLOR: Your scanner is kinda biting. My palm is still itching a bit.
GIRL: Because of this exact process. Are you hurt?
SAYLOR: (looking at her palm) No-no, I’m fine, don’t mind me.
GIRL: If you are, there’s a medical kit not far away, I may assist you medically.
SAYLOR: No need, really. I can manage—
GIRL: (grabs Saylor by the wrist, palm facing up) No, let me take a look. Uh… Mom, why are you so stubborn?
SAYLOR: I was just a little stung. Well, a hundred times, seems like. But hey, if there were any needles in that… _bloody_ interface as my friend would put it, they were incredibly thin, I didn’t even feel anything.
GIRL: Mom. With this logic, any enemy could beat you by shooting poison darts at your neck, and you would faceplant onto the table with false satisfaction.
SAYLOR: That makes me look tough, don’t you think?

The girl shakes her head side to side disapprovingly.

SAYLOR: Maybe a little bit?
GIRL: Your hand is like a sieve now. A blood sieve.
SAYLOR: These cuts are ridiculously narrow! They’ll heal in half a week! Don’t bother!
GIRL: I don’t accept it. This is not how my mother is supposed to behave! Besides, you must know perfectly well that the air outside is not sterile enough. Don’t wait for complications to arise. You’ll get a proper treatment.

The girl pulls Saylor’s arm and drags her in the direction towards the first-aid room.

SAYLOR: Oh, good. I’m owned by a child now. Ow, careful! I own my own hands!
GIRL: See? Caught red-handed! You’re in pain.
SAYLOR: I’m not! (higher-pitched, voice cracks a bit) You’re pulling me too hard! Let go!

Change of the scene. First-aid room. Two of them are sitting at the table. The girl treats Saylor’s wounds with medical serum. Minor wounds heal right before the eyes in seconds.

GIRL: You know. A child of my type has an obligation to resolve the harm caused to their parents as a result of DNA generation procedure. Be it something heavy or… your case.
SAYLOR: I’m not familiar with the local rules. You probably can’t account for an arm chopped off.
GIRL: Don’t refer to the outdated 87th-century practices.
SAYLOR: Took my mental notes to never go there, thanks.
GIRL: Funny that you’re a time traveler. You should be over 11,000 years old by now. Feeling old yet?
SAYLOR: So you know.
GIRL: DNA. And a blazing fast connection to the brain.
SAYLOR: (quietly) Yeah-yeah, don’t remind me.
GIRL: Charlotte. I’m looking forward to meet her.
SAYLOR: I’ll tell her. She has… special views on time travel.
GIRL: But it all stays between you and me.
SAYLOR: And that computer I touched doesn’t record anything? No telemetry? No middlemen?
GIRL: Computers don’t care about human population here anymore. Computers in this room only serve to transfer the information from one person to another. There’s no leak, no connection to the outside world.

Saylor snorted at the word “leak.”

GIRL: Except if you go out, every person there is transparent by default. Every secret is known, so we don’t have secrets. That’s how our society works. And still, we have private spaces like this, where nobody from the outside can hear us.
SAYLOR: I have mixed feelings about this whole concept.
GIRL: No wonder. (Saylor tries to move her hand, but the girl holds it more firmly) No, don’t move your hand just yet. Let the serum do the work, just for a few seconds.
SAYLOR: How do you know all of that while being mothballed in a single pod for 16 years?
GIRL: The liquid in the pod contains concentrated cultural data, constantly renewed. Basically, I lived without living.
SAYLOR: Gross. And you didn’t even have a name.
GIRL: Which brings us neatly to our main issue. Saylor. What is my name?
SAYLOR: Sigh… Okay, if you insist. Let’s call you… Stelle. Any good?
STELLE: Stelle… My name is Stelle… I’m Stelle.
SAYLOR: Stellar. (puts her healed palm on Stelle’s shoulder) Now, darling, there are three things we should do, okay?
STELLE: Name them.
SAYLOR: One. You must complete your ensemble.
STELLE: I’m covered up, as you requested—
SAYLOR: That’s not enough! I’ll help you. Two. I’m not your mother. Your mother can’t be 4 years older than you. Have some sense and… find someone more suitable.
STELLE: My DNA structure proves that I’m your biological descendant. You can’t deny science.
SAYLOR: Okay, just pretend we’re friends or sisters, that’s all. Please. Although I have to teach you acting… I hate teaching. Never mind. Three. We’re going to have dinner and talk about other stuff very, very thoroughly.
STELLE: I need to figure out my food preferences.
SAYLOR: Well, that’s the least of my concerns. Your vocabulary concerns me more. Your taste buds can adapt. My patience cannot. (gets up and points to the exit door) Go start with the point one. Now.
STELLE: (gets up) Okay, I can handle it myself. I need more green. I’ll be back in a moment, Mom.

Stelle hugs Saylor a bit too tight.

SAYLOR: Ow?
STELLE: And don’t follow me. I want it to be a surprise!

Stelle kisses Saylor on the cheek.

SAYLOR: What the hell was that for?
STELLE: I mean, isn’t it an expression of child’s love?
SAYLOR: Don’t make it weird. I may begin to enjoy it, and I’ll regret that.
STELLE: (chuckles) There’s always something new to learn. For you, it’s learning to be a parent. Get used to it.

Stelle frees Saylor and walks to the door.

SAYLOR: I’m not babysitting you! Let’s establish!

Stelle stops at the door, she has her back turned to Saylor.

SAYLOR: (crosses her arms over her chest) If we agree on my role as a mother, you will learn to be independent! We’ll have a very abridged crash course on independence! That’s going to be my girl!
STELLE: Consider it settled. I love independence.
SAYLOR: I can see how independent you are. You can’t stop for five minutes without relying on me.
STELLE: (turns her head) Oh, come on! This is my first day, cut me some slack!
SAYLOR: I see your vocabulary is improving. That’s a progress.

Stelle nods and leaves. Saylor grins and briefly chuckles.

A few moments later. Saylor sits at the table, lightly fidgeting stuff on the table, waiting patiently. Knock knock.

SAYLOR: Who’s there?
STELLE: The doctor.
SAYLOR: Doctor who?
STELLE: That’s not funny, okay?
SAYLOR: No, I asked seriously.
STELLE: I’m Stelle.
SAYLOR: Good. Have you finished?
STELLE: See for yourself. Are you ready?
SAYLOR: Shoot.

This time, Stelle got a bright green sundress with belt and wide skirt. She has green shoes with low heels, a couple of bracelets, a smartwatch, and triangular earrings. Her brown hair remains long and wavy.

SAYLOR: (slowly gets up in shock) Oh. My. Shooting stars. Is this legal?
STELLE: What… do you mean? Do you like it? Or is there something off? Probably green needs to be toned down, or…
SAYLOR: (excited) This is… this is… oh, incredible. I wouldn’t have enough confidence to pull this off. But you did. Look at you. Bright and loud. You can’t be serious.
STELLE: Why can’t I be?
SAYLOR: (holding Stelle’s shoulders) Are you kidding? My girl, you’re beautiful! To think that you’re _kind of_ my daughter, my past self wouldn’t believe you or… even myself. If there’s anyone who’s gonna criticize your look, I’d personally obliterate them from existence. Or set the sleepwalkers on them.
STELLE: Right on cue. We have a business to discuss, concerning them. I must audit your equipment.
SAYLOR: I know, I know. I’m properly equipped, though, don’t worry. (wraps her arm around Stelle’s shoulder as they both walk to the exit) Point three.
STELLE: Yes, Mom.
SAYLOR: I guess I’ll give up on the second one.
