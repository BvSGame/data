# Drink!

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **July 20–21, 2026** by **liledix⁴**. This is a follow-up to _A Delicate Trouble_.

----

2024, Saylor’s rental. The kitchen.

Saylor and Stelle are sitting at the table. Stelle is still dressed in her newly established outfit: green sundress with wide skirt, waist belt, some bracelets on one hand, a smartwatch on another, triangular earrings, low-heeled green shoes. Her long brown wavy hair is neatly fixed thanks to Saylor.

Stelle tries some bubblegum soda from the bottle. She wrinkles and swallows very hard.

STELLE: Ugh! Eugh!
SAYLOR: What is it? Don’t tell me you didn’t like it. Or… are you hurt?
STELLE: (holds the bottle up to eye level, looks at it, then looks at Saylor) Mom, is it necessary for a drink to have carbonation?
SAYLOR: Seriously, Stelle? Is carbonation the biggest obstacle in enjoying a beverage now?
STELLE: It spoils the taste. I barely feel the drink itself, only the offensive effect of carbon dioxide. Cough-cough. Oh my tongue.
SAYLOR: Darling, lots of drinks are like that. I wouldn’t advise you to try alcohol, that’s far worse.
STELLE: Notes taken. It’s defined, I’m an extremely steadfast teetotaler.
SAYLOR: (half-voiced) Until the first Aperol spritz, of course.
STELLE: Is there any softer drink?
SAYLOR: Tea? Yeah, let’s proceed with tea.

Saylor gets up from the table and walks over to the sink to fill the electric kettle with water. Then she opens the kitchen cabinet to get two tea bags. She puts one tea bag in each mug.

STELLE: (puts the bottle on the table) Let me assume. Your tea is bubblegum-flavored too.

Saylor walks over to the table to put mugs, then she goes back to the sink.

SAYLOR: Right first time.
STELLE: At least it won’t be gas that ruins the experience.

Saylor returns with the kettle and puts it on the stand.

SAYLOR: No gas. Only electric kettle.

Stelle flips the switch.

STELLE: Is Aperol spritz green?
SAYLOR: It’s called Midori spritz. (instantly covers her mouth)
STELLE: Ooh—
SAYLOR: (waves her hands) Hey hey hey, stop right there. Remember, we’re in a family-friendly setting.
STELLE: (breaks the fourth wall, looking at the viewer) Oops.
SAYLOR: And I’m 20, so don’t even involve me in this conversation.
STELLE: (deadpan sarcastic) Alternatively, over 11,000.
SAYLOR: In your era, probably. In mine, it’s different. I’m born in 2003. Do the math.
STELLE: But it’s 21.
SAYLOR: It _will_ be 21 this year, later, in August 30th. And what is the current month?
STELLE: April.
SAYLOR: So I’m 20. Yet.
STELLE: How do you know about these drinks, then?
SAYLOR: I’m not a caveman, you know!
STELLE: Okay, okay! But still.
SAYLOR: Sigh… (a bit ashamed of her memories) My… stepmother. Jenna.
STELLE: What about her?
SAYLOR: When I was like you, she forced me into movie nights every damn day because she was bored. While I wasn’t.
STELLE: Heh, you do look like a person who never gets bored.
SAYLOR: I had my garage where I assembled little robots out of toy scraps. Nice times… Anyways, you could tell how much brands and naming you could learn from movies alone while not drinking a single drop ever.
STELLE: Really? Is your… entertainment media _that_ corrupted?
SAYLOR: I wouldn’t say that. Brainrot memes do worse.
STELLE: Brain-what?
SAYLOR: Don’t mind it. Well, I’m sure Scarlett is pretty experienced, but… she’s responsible. She’ll never tell you a thing.
STELLE: 5 years for me to the Mido—
SAYLOR: Stop!
STELLE: Sorry. In fact, in the 14th millennium, we have age acceleration rooms.
SAYLOR: That’s a bad cheat code. Do you wanna outrace me?
STELLE: Only applicable to my type of the living organisms. If _you_ try, you die, since your body is not designed to be reprogrammed.
SAYLOR: And yours is?
STELLE: Well, it was artificially designed for me to be your daughter the moment you interacted with the pod interface.
SAYLOR: In this case, let’s establish the family rules. You don’t take my car to go accelerating your age, alright?
STELLE: Mom, your rules are absurd.
SAYLOR: Deal with it, we’re time travelers, it’s in our DNA.
STELLE: That’s one thing you’re not wrong about. Well, metaphorically.
SAYLOR: Cease the semantics speech. In this house, we always talk metaphorically.
STELLE: You said it metaphorically.
SAYLOR: (lightly pleading) Please don’t play clever, it makes me feel dumb.
STELLE: Oh hey, your liquid finished doing the pod thing.
SAYLOR: Ew, Stelle. We’re actually at the dinner table. Mind your language.
STELLE: Better don’t open the lid. I might be getting a sister.
SAYLOR: This is just a 2024 kitchen appliance!
STELLE: I mean… It doesn’t have sensors, right? Is your hand fine?
SAYLOR: Girl, you’re adopting my worst traits. It makes me jealous.

Saylor pours boiling water from an electric kettle into mugs containing tea bags, then drops two sugar cubes into her mug. Saylor was about to pick up one more cube to drop it into Stelle’s mug, but changed her mind.

STELLE: Jealous… Jellous…
SAYLOR: Your vocabulary is slipping.
STELLE: No, it doesn’t. I want some Jell-O.

Now it’s Stelle who gets up from the table.

SAYLOR: Hey! If tea won’t be sweet enough, we have sugar. Just saying.

Stelle walks to the fridge. Not the vintage one (it’s in the SUV trunk) but the modern one.

SAYLOR: How do your mood swings even work?
STELLE: I bet you can’t explain it yourself.
SAYLOR: I can, but usually it ends with me making flowcharts—
STELLE: Which we’re not doing today. Ooh, look at the physics of this object!

Stelle takes a plate of green Jell-O out of the fridge, then she grabs two spoons and returns to the table.

STELLE: Wobble-wobble-wobble.
SAYLOR: Physician in the room…
STELLE: The apple doesn’t fall far from the tree. Especially if it’s a green one.
SAYLOR: I prefer peachy pink.

Stelle sits down. She was about to grab her mug of tea, but Saylor stopped her.

SAYLOR: Hey. It’s still hot. Fizz is a minor problem, but you could burn your tongue here. With respect, I don’t think our emergency medical services are competent enough to handle artificially grown humans.
STELLE: You talk too much. The water has cooled down.

Stelle sips some tea. She pauses, then quickly finishes her tea. Mixed expression on her face is palpable.

STELLE: It’s… weird, but I’ll accept it.

Stelle was about to eat the tea bag too, but Saylor snatched it.

SAYLOR: No sugar?
STELLE: (pokes the Jell-O) Why, sugar is here. Compared to your little cubes, it wobbles.
SAYLOR: Um. Where did you get this Jell-O, anyway?
STELLE: Convenience store.
SAYLOR: And money?
STELLE: I took yours. Well, okay, I borrowed.
SAYLOR: Stelle. You could ask.
STELLE: I’ll acknowledge that. But you know, for my defense, I was being pragmatic.
SAYLOR: Pragmatic?
STELLE: You’ll see.
SAYLOR: I bet this is not bubblegum flavored.
STELLE: Kiwi flavored. It smells incredible already.

Stelle takes a deep breath of the kiwi Jell-O’s scent, then slaps the table with both hands.

STELLE: I’m about to ban bubblegum flavor here.
SAYLOR: This is _my_ rental!
STELLE: Either kiwi wins or I’ll wish to get back in my pod for the rest of my lifetime.
SAYLOR: You don’t blackmail me so casually.
STELLE: (sticks the spoon in the Jell-O) Watch me.
SAYLOR: (calm, confident, intrigued tone, with smirk) Okay, you rebellious kid. Take your cheap wins where you can get them. I’ll look how far you’ll get with this.
STELLE: Now hush. True culinary aesthetics begin. Watch and learn.
SAYLOR: The audacity.
STELLE: I said, hush! I took two spoons for a reason. As long as you’re chewing, you can’t perform verbal counterattack. I won’t allow myself to be vulnerable. Your money, your Jell-O, so chew on.

Saylor takes a spoonful of green gelatin.

SAYLOR: What am I, a living furniture?
STELLE: A multiversal taxi driver.

Saylor chokes a little, but manages to clear her throat.

SAYLOR: If you weren’t my descendant, I would throw you into open space the first chance I get.
STELLE: Rough way of free roaming. Very lethal.
SAYLOR: Trust the 1980s SUV time machine user.

Saylor eats another piece of kiwi Jell-O and washes it down with bubblegum tea.

STELLE: Mom. Wait…
SAYLOR: What is it?
STELLE: I feel something strange…
SAYLOR: Tell me. Tell me now.
STELLE: My… eyelids?
SAYLOR: Don’t dramatize. Your eyelids are fine.
STELLE: No. No… They’re… heavy…

Stelle rolls her eyes and sways a little.

SAYLOR: Stelle! What’s going on?!
STELLE: I… I never experienced… What is this?…
SAYLOR: Stelle!!
STELLE: I need… I need…
SAYLOR: Tell me what you need, quickly!
STELLE: I need… a soft landing…
SAYLOR: What?!
STELLE: H… Help me, Mom… I can’t… I can’t fight it, Mom, help…

Stelle falls off the chair. Saylor panics and runs out to her.

SAYLOR: Are you poisoned? Do you know if you’re poisoned or not?
STELLE: (whispering, slowly) I’m not… Trust my product composition analysis… That Jell-O was safe… The tea was safe… This is… this is some… natural weakness…
SAYLOR: Stelle, please!

Stelle exhales, relaxes, and passes out. All Saylor can see in what remains of Stelle’s consciousness is a very slow inhalation and exhalation.

SAYLOR: (grabs Stelle’s shoulders and shakes her lightly) Stelle, can you hear me?

Dead to the world. No reaction, just inhaling and exhaling very slowly.

SAYLOR: You little dork. You can’t control your sleep deprivation! That’s what it is.

Saylor tries to pull Stelle by the shoulders to lift her up. It’s uneasy.

SAYLOR: Uh… 110 pounds of my patience shattering…

Finally, Saylor managed to wrap one arm around Stelle’s neck, lift Stelle’s body with her own weight, and wrap her other arm around Stelle’s legs to carry her.

SAYLOR: 16 years of not knowing the biological needs of your own organism. Now learn it the hard way. Every time you need a nap, the table will slap your stupid smart face.

With great effort, Saylor tries to carry Stelle to the couch in the adjacent living room.

SAYLOR: (looking at the viewer) Is this the kind of parenting I unwittingly signed up for?…
