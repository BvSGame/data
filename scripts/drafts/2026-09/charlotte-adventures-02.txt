# Charlotte Adventures: Part 2

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **September 4, 2026** by **liledix⁴**. Edited in **September 7, 2026**.

37th century. 7 PM. 300 yards from the building illuminated by floodlights.

Flash! Bang! CHARLOTTE and RICK materialized. They both wear the same outfit: white blazers, black button-up shirts, white bowties, black pants. They even took care of the hairstyles, CHARLOTTE temporarily traded her curls for wavy hair.

CHARLOTTE: Ah! (deep breath) Such fresh air! That’s unusual.
RICK: Even though it seems like we’re in the city.
CHARLOTTE: Not every metropolis has an insufferable odor.
RICK: Not even in the future. I see.
CHARLOTTE: You’re a bit biased, Ricky-Rick, too on display.
RICK: Wh— Ricky-Rick?
CHARLOTTE: Yes! Works as the diminutive, right?
RICK: Saylor never called me like that… I don’t even know who ever did.
CHARLOTTE: Blooming heck, I came up with the unique diminutive?!
RICK: You really did. (wondering) Yeah… Yeah, you actually did.
CHARLOTTE: (gasps) Noted and reserved!
RICK: Charry, we have a task ahead.
CHARLOTTE: Ah! Right.
RICK: Saylor pointed out that we have to find a secret agent from one of the groups affiliated with Darkrock, but keep ourselves unannounced.
CHARLOTTE: Which means, we’re blending in with the crowd in a public event, there’s a social activity in that building we’re heading to. VIPs only. Has Lisa created fake identities?
RICK: (checks his phone) Uh… Yeah, she did. But my phone. It’s from the 2020s. Wouldn’t it be a problem during security checks?
CHARLOTTE: Rick, please. It’s a social event, not a bank!
RICK: 16 centuries in the future. Over a millennium and a half. Technology changes.
CHARLOTTE: You will pass, don’t worry. The thing is, sunshine, we’re not arriving empty-handed.
RICK: You have a plan?
CHARLOTTE: Yep. Right for this scenario, I wrote down some instructions in advance. Hang on… (rummages through her pants pocket, pulls out a notepad) Here. If I remember correctly, it’s uh… page 93.
RICK: (takes notepad, begins to flip the pages) Well, let’s see— (pauses, astonished) You’ve got to be kidding me… So that’s why you chose notepads over digital notes…
CHARLOTTE: What caught your eye?
RICK: Your handwriting!!
CHARLOTTE: Amazing, isn’t it? I like it myself! (RICK keeps flipping the pages) Had to spend weeks refining it while writing letters in 1837 London. Well, not strictly London. Cambridge as well. Oh, and Reading. The list goes on, honestly.
RICK: Reading?
CHARLOTTE: Town in Berkshire. Writing in Reading, sounds brilliant! Wanna visit?
RICK: (focused on the notes) Would be nice, yeah…
CHARLOTTE: (operates PTT) Good, pre-setting it right now, it’s gonna be our next destination after this mission.
RICK: (stops at page 93, skims through) Well, your handwriting is certainly better than what you’re about to suggest for a plan!
CHARLOTTE: You could choose a better compliment.
RICK: Well, let me read it aloud.
CHARLOTTE: Oh my, you’re actually going to read my own handwriting for me!
RICK: Focus, Char! Now. “We must act more seriously than usual and not give away a single clue about our true selves.”
CHARLOTTE: Baseline stuff for staying incognito.
RICK: “If there’s a buffet, be sure to grab a bite to eat every 3 minutes.”
CHARLOTTE: I mean, you have to make an impression that you’re a valid part of what’s been organized.
RICK: “During a conversation, if it’s not possible to keep the conversation going without jeopardizing the mission, you should nod at the end of each remark and let out a small chuckle at the jokes.”
CHARLOTTE: Validating interlocutors to achieve a false sense of security.
RICK: “As soon as the conversation turns to relationships, pretend you’re a couple”—now I have questions.
CHARLOTTE: What? Worried that we won’t fit in?
RICK: Why can’t we act natural when it comes to this particular item on the list?
CHARLOTTE: Because it won’t be believable! We have to act like we’re together because people will definitely notice that we communicate too often, we meet too often, we share the table—
RICK: Okay, okay, I get it. Basically you’re saying that this is the most believable cover story that requires the least effort.
CHARLOTTE: You understand it perfectly yourself!
RICK: When do we get started?
CHARLOTTE: Let’s not waste our time.
RICK: Think carefully. What if we must wait until enough people arrive? I mean, if we appear in the scene too early while there aren’t that many people, we become more transparent for that hiding agent.
CHARLOTTE: You’re right. We must wait. 20 more minutes, and we act.
RICK: That’s more like it.

## P.S.

2024, the roof of the apartment building where RICK lives. 1 AM.

RICK, in casual clothes, is sitting by the railing, looking up at the stars.

CHARLOTTE: (echoing from the back) Piercing the fabric of the heavens with your gaze so that new stars will appear, eh?

RICK turns around. CHARLOTTE is there, wearing casual 21st-century sundress.

RICK: Silent appearance? Is that really you?
CHARLOTTE: Stealth mode. Consumes more—
RICK: —more energy, but allows you to materialize without flash and without bang. I’ve figured out. Sigh… You don’t even need any comments. You’ve already heard everything I could ever say. Everything else is just variations on what’s already been said.
CHARLOTTE: (sitting by the railing, joining RICK’s company) Why sudden change in tone? I don’t see a person who visited Saylor’s rental in 2024, the Resistance HQ in 2038, the 22nd-century Paris, the pompous 37th-century gala, quiet library of 1837 Berkshire, and a small tea party with Lavender, all in one day.
RICK: Charley. Lately, I haven’t been spending more time with anyone else than with you. That’s not normal.
CHARLOTTE: Not normal? Why?
RICK: I’ve spent 12 years of my life with Saylor.
CHARLOTTE: Ah, an itch that is old as life…
RICK: And I’m afraid these years are gradually slipping away under the pressure you’re putting on me.
CHARLOTTE: Pressure… Fine. Let’s address that. Are you really sure that _I_ am the problem? What if _you_ want to hide away? Hm?

RICK is silent.

CHARLOTTE: You think I wouldn’t notice that you want to do this even now? Hide from everyone and get buried in your own thoughts? Rick. With respect, your head is a very noisy place, and I know that very well. That’s why I’m here. To stop the noise.
RICK: Stop the noise, hm?
CHARLOTTE: Well, everyone is trying! Everyone. Because we care about you as much as you care about us. You see that perfectly! But you refuse us. You find comfort in focusing on yourself. But what happens if you just give us a chance? Do you really think you’re _that_ vulnerable? You don’t want to show your biggest, deepest cracks because you’re afraid of getting shattered for good?
RICK: (lowers his head) I don’t know anymore. I just don’t know. (pauses) You’re talking smoothly. My soul refuses to believe you, guys. (chin up) It’s my mission. My road to go.
CHARLOTTE: You don’t even know where you’re going. You don’t have a map. You follow the instincts. That’s not a reliable way.
RICK: And you have? A map? Do you have it?
CHARLOTTE: Blooming heck, you can’t be serious right now! How many times did we travel together? You still think I’m navigating blindly?

RICK is silent.

CHARLOTTE: Rick. I’m taking you with me so you can pay attention to other people’s lives. So you can realize just how diverse people can be. And one day—maybe one day—you’ll open your eyes and notice that all those mental barriers you’ve built were nothing more than meaningless, unsubstantial sounds you let fill your head because you were afraid of the silence. Silence consumes you? Counterattack it. Feed it with joy. We’re here for it. _I_ am here for it.
RICK: It would drive you insane. See what happened to Marie when she tried to fix me. Where is she now? Pining for me in the corners of the Agency because “if you can’t use a screwdriver, use a sledgehammer?”
CHARLOTTE: Don’t you dare compare me to her. More than that, who told you I’m not ready to smash my head? You know perfectly well what kinds of risks I’m drawn to. Remember I said “I don’t take ‘no’ for an answer?”
RICK: Yes.
CHARLOTTE: So allow me to take the risks. I consented to this.
RICK: (sighs, laughs nervously) You’re unstoppable. You don’t even know how dangerous my head can be. It would break all of you. You would hope you’ve never met me.
CHARLOTTE: And that really puts my resilience to the test. I’m serious, Rick. I agreed to this, and you won’t be able to change my mind. I can jump through space and time as much as I want, but remember. I’m not going anywhere.

CHARLOTTE claps RICK’s shoulder and slowly walks away. At some moment she stops.

CHARLOTTE: Not until you see what I want you to see.

And she keeps walking away.

RICK stares at the stars for another 10 seconds, then some idea hits him. His eyes widen, and he runs toward the stairs.

Change of the scene to the garage in the SAYLOR’s rental house. 1:40 AM. We see RICK’s silhouette against the glow of streetlights as the garage door slowly opens.

A few minutes later. The light is low. RICK passionately works on some complicated big device. Behind him, LISA enters the garage.

LISA: Rick? May I ask what are you doing here?
RICK: (startled) Ah!! Lisa?! What are you doing here? Ah, right, you asked first.
LISA: Correct. So?
RICK: I’ve got a sudden idea to enhance this piece of machinery for a quest this morning. Your turn.
LISA: While you were busy with Charlotte for almost entire day, Saylor took her SUV to 2038 and we landed here together. Because she needed my physical assistance in 2024.
RICK: Makes sense. Um… Any reason for your visit?
LISA: Casual check-in.
RICK: Lissie, dear. We both know that you never do “casual.” You can literally explain your every step with a flowchart.
LISA: I wish you never tried to be more analytical than me.
RICK: Makes you jealous?
LISA: Makes your attempt look laughable, and I hate to see it.
RICK: Savage…
LISA: Rick. I have a confession available. Can I express it?
RICK: Ahem… Lisa.
LISA: Yes?
RICK: People say “I want to confess.”
LISA: Ah. Ha-ha. Affirmative. I mixed up my vocabularies. I offer my deepest apologies.
RICK: What’s the confession?
LISA: You give a permission?
RICK: Yes, I’m listening. Spit it out.
LISA: I can’t. I am technically incapable of having salivary glands.
RICK: (embarrassed) No! I didn’t mean it literally! Put your cards on the table. Cut to the chase. Stop beating around the bush.
LISA: That’s too many variables…
RICK: Liz!
LISA: (playfully pinches RICK’s arm) Fine, fine, Rickstar, I’m teasing you. I had thesaurus of English idioms loaded in cache this whole time. You request me to stop prevaricating.
RICK: You lunatic…
LISA: Here goes the confession… I… was human once.
RICK: Sorry?
LISA: All the Human Plus antics aside. I’m serious. I had my real, organic previous life.
RICK: Elizabeth. This is sci-fi even I refuse to take seriously. Try again.
