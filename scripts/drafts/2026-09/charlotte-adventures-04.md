# Charlotte Adventures: Part 4 (Unfinished)

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **September 13–15, 2026** by **liledix⁴**.

----

2345, streets.

Flash! Bang! Guess who is appearing? Obviously, CHARLOTTE and RICK. And as usual, their arms are linked, because this is the way group time travel with PTT works.

As soon as they appeared, CHARLOTTE starts walking quickly and confidently as if she knew the precise destination. RICK, still linked to her arm, has to follow.

RICK: Okay Charlotte, drop the act. Why have you brought me here? 
CHARLOTTE: I’m serious, this is a surprise! And you’ll love it.
RICK: Char, listen. When you say you have a surprise, normally this is a reason to panic, run away, and forget that you have a concept of surprises.
CHARLOTTE: Am I that terrible at surprises?
RICK: When it’s not something that may potentially kill, no. Lethal surprises happen more often, though.
CHARLOTTE: I don’t know, it adds some spice.
RICK: Charley. People don’t want spice. People want to eat. There’s a distinction. If we keep going with this metaphor, I’m afraid your taste preferences have been ruined by your love of spicy food.
CHARLOTTE: By spicy food you mean my adventures.
RICK: Yes. Precisely those.
CHARLOTTE: I’ll take it as a compliment.
RICK: Sigh… You’re impossible.
CHARLOTTE: Right? You’re overthinking. You don’t pay attention to where we’re going.
RICK: I know this era, it’s 2345, and we already tried out some things here and there when Saylor was here.
CHARLOTTE: The era doesn’t matter. We are going to relax. The place we’re heading to is the place we never tried before. And you would never guess what it is. Don’t even ask anything, just trust me.
RICK: It’s funny. We saw so many places already. I can remember a hundred off the top of my head. And yet, there are still new places.
CHARLOTTE: This is time travel. Normally you hardly would be able to visit every corner of Earth. Try to also keep up with all places on Earth at every point in time. That’s impossible. 
RICK: Thankfully we pick strictly limited places.
CHARLOTTE: That’s the only way.
RICK: Ahem… Look. I don’t want to sound rude or weird, but. Is this a date?
CHARLOTTE: Pardon?
RICK: You know. Are we just traveling or is there a very specific reason why you’re choosing me as a companion almost always?
CHARLOTTE: Uh… Okay, I didn’t see this coming, honestly.
RICK: Really?
CHARLOTTE: Well. It’s just natural to pick you. I don’t even question it. Whenever I choose others, there’s always something off. Maybe they’re not competent enough. Maybe their schedule is messed up. Maybe they’re busy building robots. Maybe they’re simply boring. Of all people, why are you always a perfect balance?
RICK: Saylor’s company. You would learn everything.
CHARLOTTE: Doesn’t work with everyone. But it worked with you.
RICK: So I’m competent, my schedule is not a mess, I don’t build robots, and I’m not boring.
CHARLOTTE: Don’t take my words too literally.
RICK: Yeah, anyways.
CHARLOTTE: And yes. Consider it a date.
RICK: What? Wait, are you serious now?
CHARLOTTE: What am I going to lose, though? Well, except… Maybe I’ll lose a friend. If you understand what I mean.
RICK: You won’t.
CHARLOTTE: No-no. I meant in a sense of… _“status upgrade.”_
RICK: That was rather quick.
CHARLOTTE: Unlike you, I make a decision quickly and I stand by it. If you notice, I’m still in one piece.
RICK: That’s reassuring.
CHARLOTTE: And I won’t allow you to backpedal. If you said “date,” this is a date. There’s no way back. (pushes on RICK’s back) Come along and don’t ask questions.

5 minutes later.

RICK: So, tell me where we’re going.
CHARLOTTE: Cafeteria!
RICK: You? Cafeteria? You don’t do cafeterias.
CHARLOTTE: See? Surprise is working!
RICK: Jokes on you, I’ve already visited every cafeteria in this town.
CHARLOTTE: Who said that this is a final destination?
RICK: Right. Pit stop before the real bomb drops.
CHARLOTTE: Exactly. Also, no spicy food involved. Just something sweet. A futuristic mint cheesecake, for instance. Do you like sweets?
RICK: Are you asking? You and Saylor know it better than anyone else.
CHARLOTTE: I know. My question was rhetorical.
RICK: I’d like some coffee cake, honestly. With a pinch of… damn, I keep forgetting how that unusual green fruit was called… That one that has a strong flavor in a single gram.
CHARLOTTE: Boy, I can’t remember it either… Neither it was mentioned in the menu. Might google it later, remind me to do that.
RICK: That’s the problem with the future. They don’t explain the ingredients.
CHARLOTTE: Well, about your choice of cake, I’m not surprised. That’s your way to avoid _drinking_ coffee separately. “Why can’t I have coffee and cake merged and eat it too?”
RICK: (laughs) Stop it.
CHARLOTTE: No, I won’t.
RICK: Okay, okay, if there’s anything that could stop you.
CHARLOTTE: Malaria in the 9th century, but we’re not talking about that.
RICK: (serious) Stop it.
CHARLOTTE: Yeah, right. Well, the interesting thing is, I may have spent 15 years of my life in the 59th century, but this is still the far future for me.
RICK: I mean, considering how deeply the 19th century integrated into your DNA, I’m not surprised. That is what defined your worldview.
CHARLOTTE: You see right through it. So! That’s the cafeteria!
RICK: Ladies first.
CHARLOTTE: Nah, I don’t do manners outside the relevant setting. After you.
RICK: Right.

As they enter…

CHARLOTTE: Do you know why I’m next?
RICK: I’m terrified to know.
CHARLOTTE: Imagine this is a video game. I like to imagine this is a third-person view of a handsome character. Close-up on the back of the head.
RICK: Stop it.
CHARLOTTE: Well, that one character who has bad skill set but you can’t resist watching them from behind.
RICK: I said, stop it.
CHARLOTTE: Fine, fine. Do I look weird lagging behind?
RICK: Charley. My head is about to short-circuit.
CHARLOTTE: Good!

Cafeteria. RICK and CHARLOTTE are sitting at the table. CHARLOTTE drinks tea (the usual) while RICK sips coffee.

CHARLOTTE: You think we’ve just visited cafeteria, hm?

CHARLOTTE removes PTT from her forearm and puts it on the table.

RICK: No. No-no-no-no-no. Absolutely not. This is your transport!
CHARLOTTE: Yours now.
RICK: I refuse!
CHARLOTTE: Come on. You might remember a hundred different locations off the top of your head. Make it a thousand.
RICK: Charlotte. I will not wear this.
CHARLOTTE: Hm. Alright.

Few seconds of pause. PTT is still on the table.

CHARLOTTE: Still drinking your coffee?
RICK: Well. My cake is still being made. Why does it take this long? Normally in this century it’s pop and done.
CHARLOTTE: You claim you’ve visited every cafeteria in the town, yet you’re still wondering about one place where every cake is handmade by design. I chose this specific cafeteria deliberately because handicraft adds some flair to the atmosphere, don’t you think?
RICK: I know, I know.
CHARLOTTE: So why rushing? You literally have all time and space at your fingertips, and you’re worried that making some cake takes ten more minutes. Just lean back on the sofa and wait.
RICK: Funny to hear that from your mouth. You’re the most hyperactive one.
CHARLOTTE: Only because I have a choice. I can choose action, I can choose relaxation, and switching between those doesn’t break the schedule. In 2038, at our Resistance HQ, I can plan something for the next hour and come back to it after a week, and you won’t even notice anything wrong. Right before some Arctic expedition, I could’ve gone to a summer pool party, who knows.
RICK: Life of a time traveler. Must be a chaos to remember your every promise.
CHARLOTTE: I just stated it’s possible. I try not to procrastinate. Because give your laziness a chance to shift your tasks, and a week later you’ll see a pile of tasks you’re psychologically unprepared to handle. An unconsumable plate.
RICK: As a college student, I’m aware.

RICK finishes his coffee. As soon as he puts the cup on the saucer, CHARLOTTE quickly grabs RICK’s hand and wraps PTT on it.

RICK: No, Char!
CHARLOTTE: Don’t remove it. I’m serious, take it. I absolutely need you to take it.
RICK: Why?
CHARLOTTE: You can’t see, huh? Sunshine, if this is how we’ve settled our shared time, this is how we make a time travel initiation. And this is what I’m willing to gift to the most trusted person. You’re far from being a random person, Rick. You’ve literally saved my life. A lot.
RICK: You could give me time.
CHARLOTTE: Silly! Blooming heck, I literally gave it to you! And I don’t mean just PTT. We know each other for long months. Which, with my style of life and a fairly firm philosophical stance, equals long years. You’re really going to tell me this is a controversial choice, aren’t you?
RICK: How am I supposed to react to this?
CHARLOTTE: Acceptance is all I need.
RICK: I can’t.
CHARLOTTE: But what are you losing?
RICK: What about you? How would you travel?
CHARLOTTE: Who said I’ll stay empty-handed?

CHARLOTTE demonstratively pulls out her closed multi-functional parasol, currently designed as a simple umbrella.

RICK: You keep the parasol.
CHARLOTTE: While you take PTT.
RICK: But parasol just collects power!
CHARLOTTE: And you can’t travel without that power. See where this goes?
RICK: What the hell? Is this your idea of… proper time-travel duo?
CHARLOTTE: You hinted at an evolving relationship. This is how the next stage works. And if you refuse to accept that, we’ll stay in 2345 until you make up your mind.
RICK: Blackmail…
CHARLOTTE: Collaboration.
RICK: Where’s that damn cake?
CHARLOTTE: Should be done in two minutes, I believe. Check PTT. I’ve registered the progress tracking.
RICK: So that’s how Saylor ordered stuff…
CHARLOTTE: Exactly. If your gadget is registered in a compatible billing system, you may see live report of how something is being cooked. Or delivered. Or whatever service you use. Convenient, eh?
RICK: That’s so 2345.
CHARLOTTE: And you wanted to criticize my surprise. Take that.

## P.S.

1838, London palace. Back to Part 1 postscript events.

8:30 AM. RICK’s guest room. RICK sleeps calmly, dead as a doornail.

Someone knocks on the door. RICK doesn’t react. Knocks continued.

????: (muffled) Rick, please open, it’s me!! Are you there?

RICK slowly wakes up and gets up from bed. A few seconds later, as he puts on clothes.

????: (muffled, knocking again) Rick, breakfast time. Can you open, please?
RICK: I’m coming!

RICK opens the door. It’s LAVENDER.

LAVENDER: Good morning!
RICK: Good morning, Lavender. It’s either not breakfast time yet or my table clock is faulty. We agreed to—
LAVENDER: Meet at 9, I know. We have to meet a little earlier.
RICK: Why?
LAVENDER: Private talk. And a little surprise. Come along, I’ll wait for you in the great hall.

LAVENDER leaves.

RICK: That was short. Hm! (walks back into the room, pauses) Which means… the real ambush is yet to happen. Whatever, I’m rambling to myself. It’s alright, Rick, you just need an anchor…

RICK leaves the room. He notices the door to CHARLOTTE’s room is open. He knocks on the door out of politeness. Then he enters and notices that there’s nobody in the room.

RICK: What? Little morning devil…

RICK goes back to his room to prepare himself.

A few minutes later. RICK enters the great hall. The servants are running back and forth, but of all the nobles, only LAVENDER can be seen, standing by the dining table.

LAVENDER: Rick. May I say, you look exceptionally gorgeous today.
RICK: You would always say that, let’s be real.
LAVENDER: Nevertheless, does it mean I’m dishonest?
RICK: No. Just overly focused.
LAVENDER: I don’t see an issue. Would you care for some strawberries?
RICK: I wouldn’t say no, that’s very kind of you.

RICK sits down. LAVENDER still stands by the dining table.

LAVENDER: My favorite kind of berries.
RICK: Lavender with strawberries. Do you even realize how it sounds?
LAVENDER: (gasps) I never noticed. That’s beautiful! Very observant, Rick, very observant. I must remember to write it down.
RICK: Are you writing?
LAVENDER: A diary, like every self-respecting lady of the nobility. But, there’s more than that.

As RICK unsuspiciously eats strawberries, LAVENDER puts the folded letter on the table.

RICK: A letter?
LAVENDER: This is for you. Read it later, at your own pace, there’s no need to rush. Just… don’t ignore. I made a bit of an effort.
RICK: Well. I don’t dare to ignore. This is an official form of communication. Must be a serious matter.
LAVENDER: Very serious.
RICK: I understand. I’ll read it later this morning.
LAVENDER: I’ll be waiting for your response. And, you don’t have to write back. If you’re going to leave before our next meeting, could you let our staff know?
RICK: Yes, I’ll tell Miss Caroline.
LAVENDER: Miss Caroline… I should’ve expected.
RICK: Something wrong?
LAVENDER: No. Of all servants, why is it always her?
RICK: Because she’s kind. Just kind. Excuse me, I need to get ready before proper breakfast starts.
LAVENDER: I’ll be waiting here.
RICK: See you soon.

RICK takes the letter and leaves. LAVENDER slowly sits down at the table and takes a strawberry.

In the hallway, RICK accidentally meets CAROLINE.

CAROLINE: Gentleman Rick, good morning!
RICK: Good morning, Miss Caroline! How’s your day going?
CAROLINE: Very well, Mister Rick.
RICK: Just Rick. I prefer it without title.
CAROLINE: I can’t, Mister Rick, that’s a protocol of courtesy.
RICK: Right.
CAROLINE: Why are you awake this early?
RICK: Social matters.
CAROLINE: I don’t think it’s an appropriate time for social matters.
RICK: I know, I know, but it’s a specific social matter. Do you mind if I explain later?
CAROLINE: Oh please, Mister Rick, it’s always a pleasure to hear stories unfold from you.
RICK: Interesting. Couldn’t ever expect to have an audience.
CAROLINE: Apparently you have, Mister Rick.
RICK: Fine, fine. You’re repeating my name too much.
CAROLINE: Protocol of courtesy, excuse me.
RICK: Listen. Some people already stopped using “Gentleman” when they refer to me. It’s not a problem to stop using “Mister” as well.
CAROLINE: I can’t, sir. The gap in social standing is too great.
RICK: Understood. Well, I guess we’ll leave it for another day.
CAROLINE: Sir, may I ask if you’re planning to depart today?
RICK: Not this morning. In case of afternoon, we’ll see. I’ll let you know before my departure, of course.
CAROLINE: Sure. You know where to find me.
RICK: Oh, and. Do you know where Lady Charlotte is?
CAROLINE: Was she leaving?
RICK: The last time I checked her room, she wasn’t there. I might assume she went for a walk.
CAROLINE: I haven’t received any errands from Lady Charlotte. Surely she mustn’t be far away. However, with the wind on her shoulders, I would expect anything.
RICK: Miss Caroline, you’re doing very well.
CAROLINE: My pleasure.
RICK: Till the next time.

RICK continues to walk down the hallway.

Library. RICK met another servant.

MICHAEL: (laughs) This story is absurd, sir!
RICK: No, I mean it! There really is a city where the flavor of a small pinch of green-colored fruit feels like an absolute purest delight, total concentrated divinity.
MICHAEL: Is there any way you may transport that culinary wonder?
RICK: You consider it possible?
MICHAEL: Sir, you’re a traveler! Imagine the sensation this will cause. You can’t possibly have this opportunity and not use it.

Public hall.

RICK: And then she says: “No, Rick, this machine is supposed to be powered entirely by air.”
MADELEINE: (laughs) Sir, that’s an absurdity! How could it possibly work with such description?
RICK: Don’t ask me, I’ve been stunned myself when I heard that!
MADELEINE: If only we could possibly visualize that. This sounds like an incredibly inspiring, novel vision. Can we get a photograph? Or maybe your friend could draw it. Or maybe one of our artists could join your trip.
RICK: That’s rather a specific request.
MADELEINE: Sir, you’re known to be a traveler.

Random room. RICK opens the door and accidentally finds a group of servants.

THOMAS: Mister Rick!
ANNE: We just discussed your tales.
ROBERT: Do you have a moment to share some more?
RICK: Oh dear.
VIOLET: (behind RICK’s back) Ahem?
RICK: Oh. Good morning, Lady Violet. Do you know what time it is?
VIOLET: Gentleman Rick. It’s 9:15 o’clock. You’re late.
RICK: Don’t blame me. Charlotte is absent.
CHARLOTTE: (echoing) Speak for yourself!

CHARLOTTE and ROSE are joining VIOLET.

RICK: Char? Where in the name of sanity have you been?
CHARLOTTE: It’s a separate tale.
ROSE: Mind joining our breakfast?
VIOLET: Lavender awaits.
RICK: You really had to mention Lavender specifically.
VIOLET: You left her waiting.
RICK: Whatever, let’s just move. I don’t want to hear anything.
????: _I_ want.

As soon as RICK was about to move, he notices…

SAYLOR: You’ve got a lot of stories to tell. Traveler.
SCARLETT: Four days, Rick. You were gone for four days.
RICK: Charlotte. What have you done?
SAYLOR: Charlotte was a good girl.
CHARLOTTE: Of course I was.
SAYLOR: Now. Violet? Rose? May I join your sweet tea session?
VIOLET: Honestly, we would be glad.
ROSE: Oh certainly.
SCARLETT: And then, we take our Rick to the mission.
RICK: I’ve been waiting for it.
CHARLOTTE: Well, leisure time comes to an end.
ROSE: (to SCARLETT) Wait a second. We haven’t met before.
SCARLETT: I’m Lady Magenta. Gentleman Rick is my colleague. We’re not simply travelers. We’re fighting the threat of the clockwork men across the world.
VIOLET: Magenta… You’re not from England?
SCARLETT: Abroad. But we’re working for the good of the world. Now, Rick. Tea, Lavender, and we’re moving.
RICK: Magenta!
SCARLETT: Don’t Magenta me. You have four of _my_ off-duty evenings overdue, Rick. You promised: helping to fix my motorcycle, play billiards, experiment with the new variations of smoothies, and just have a talk that doesn’t involve operations.
RICK: Thank goodness you memorized it perfectly.

Everyone starts walking. SAYLOR stands to the left of RICK, and CHARLOTTE stands to the right.

SAYLOR: Seriously? Lavender?
RICK: Stop.
SAYLOR: No, I won’t stop, Rick. Lavender, then!
RICK: I beg you.
SAYLOR: Oh yeah, scream for mercy. I’m about to make you choke on every sip, brother.
RICK: Sa— Catherine, please!
SAYLOR: Charlie, look at this fool, you raised Casanova!
CHARLOTTE: Catherine, we can demolish him later. Let the man enjoy his tea with this lingering thought.
SAYLOR: This is the exact reason why I adore you. You know how to make terrifying out of mundane.
CHARLOTTE: So let’s just stick to it.

SAYLOR and CHARLOTTE are laughing. RICK feels embarrassed.

RICK: Charlotte. If you try any trick while united with Catherine, I swear, I will—
CHARLOTTE: What? You will what?
RICK: Marry Queen Victoria herself. Just to spite all of you.
CHARLOTTE: That’s pretty achievable.
SAYLOR: Charlie, darling, you’re too fast. Let this man succeed with Lavender and get disappointed.
CHARLOTTE: Right.
SAYLOR: After that, do whatever you want.
CHARLOTTE: Is this a blessing?
SAYLOR: Absolute.
RICK: Do I have a say on this?
SAYLOR: If I’m present, no, you don’t.
CHARLOTTE: If I’m present, you don’t have it twice.

SAYLOR and CHARLOTTE high-five.

ROSE: If I may ask what you’re talking about in your dialect?
SAYLOR: (side-hugs ROSE) My dear Lady Rose, we’re preparing our finest groom for the finest Victorian scandal in the universe. I bet you’re onboard.
RICK: _I_ am not onboard!
CHARLOTTE: The gentleman doth protest too much, me thinks!
SAYLOR gasps.
VIOLET: (turned toward CHARLOTTE) The bookworm spoke.
SAYLOR: (excited) _**STELLAR**_, Char! Very timely! (to ROSE) As for you. (claps on ROSE’s shoulder) Don’t miss a beat. Full gossip permission.
