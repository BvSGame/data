# Charlotte Adventures: Part 6

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **September 15–17, 2026** by **liledix⁴**.

----

2047, park. The classic time-travel duo is walking. CHARLOTTE, dressed in a white sundress and sneakers, and RICK, dressed in a white T-shirt and jeans. Very casual. RICK still has PTT on his forearm, properly disguised.

RICK: I can bet my soul, that figure had 71 faces.
CHARLOTTE: Don’t argue with me, I was an A student in geometry. It’s mathematically impossible for that figure to have 71 faces. The formula won’t cover entire volume.
RICK: Okay, assuming I’m really wrong, what is your suggestion?
CHARLOTTE: 73 faces.
RICK: That’s foolish, you’re just confusing it with hypertrodepocube.
CHARLOTTE: I’m totally not confusing it with hypertrodepocube. They have too many differences. You just can’t notice them, sweetheart.
RICK: I’m still standing by 71 faces. It makes sense to me.
CHARLOTTE: You know what? To hell with this. If you insist so stubbornly, let it be your way. But if we fail that quiz game in 2942, I’m going to make you a laughingstock in front of the entire mathematics community.
RICK: Which consists of three nerds anyway.
CHARLOTTE: But furious nerds.
RICK: Scary-scary-scary.
CHARLOTTE: Uh… Rick.
RICK: What?
CHARLOTTE: I think I’m hallucinating.
RICK: Meaning?
CHARLOTTE: Look.

RICK and CHARLOTTE see a mid-20s woman sitting on a bench and flipping through a book.

RICK: A woman.
CHARLOTTE: No. No, no, no. Not just a woman. No.
RICK: You know her?
CHARLOTTE: I think I recognize her… Come on.

RICK and CHARLOTTE are coming closer. As CHARLOTTE begins to recognize, she begins to beam with excitement.

CHARLOTTE: Oh of course I wasn’t wrong! It’s really you! My goodness, I haven’t seen you in years!
????: (slowly raises her head) What the—
CHARLOTTE: (spreads her own arms) Recognize me?
????: Impossible! Charlotte. The lost soul herself. How unexpected.
RICK: Good afternoon.
????: Good afternoon.
CHARLOTTE: Rick, my silly idiot, allow me to introduce. Celeste! A time traveler just like yours truly. Same century, same school, same PTT! But philosophy leaves much to be desired.
CELESTE: This comparison is raw and inaccurate, Charlotte. It lacks nuance. But honestly, you never change.
CHARLOTTE: Ah. That’s Celeste-speak for “Nice to see you.”
RICK: Yeah, I can see you know each other very, very well. What an interesting meeting.
CHARLOTTE: Still can’t apply your big academic brain to proper use? With your skills, I would bend reality like Jenesis does.
RICK: (whisper) We’re not talking about Jenesis.
CELESTE: Pardon?
CHARLOTTE: Never mind, never mind. You normally don’t interfere. Which is sometimes a good thing and sometimes not.
CELESTE: Charlotte. This is not how I imagined our reunion. Maybe you could bring a dash of purpose to our conversation?
CHARLOTTE: You treat time travel as an obligation. And that’s not even a significant part of your problem.
CELESTE: I know.
CHARLOTTE: Ricky. She’s just a conversation killer. Let’s move on.
RICK: No, wait. Charlie, can you go buy something for three of us?
CHARLOTTE: Ah, I see. Talk time with Richard! Hey, if she kills you with her boredom, let me know as soon as possible. Well, until she actually kills, of course.

CHARLOTTE walks away. RICK sits down.

RICK: You’re from the 59th century, then.
CELESTE: Yeah. And well, Charlotte was my schoolmate. Until she decided she doesn’t belong in the same era. Sorry, who are you?
RICK: I’m Rick. I’m from the beginning of 21st century. Charlotte’s most regular traveling companion. She drags me around, basically, and I accidentally become everyone’s favorite. One would hate that.
CELESTE: Celeste. A master’s degree graduate from a temporal university.
RICK: Amazing to meet a person who has a real degree in the space-time shenanigans. I suppose we must seem like absolute amateurs to you.
CELESTE: More like, I don’t pay attention.
RICK: Oh. So it doesn’t bother you.
CELESTE: Why should it? I’m just trying to live my everyday life, that’s all.
RICK: You know “everyday” is a very fluid word for a time traveler.
CELESTE: I don’t travel as much. Just occasionally running away to keep my head together after work.
RICK: After-work hobby, then.
CELESTE: Not really. You can call it therapy. Even that is not quite correct. I’m not even sure what is correct. Sorry, I shouldn’t be talking about this.
RICK: Your job… If it’s not a secret, can you tell what it is?
CELESTE: Nothing so interesting. I just sell cupcakes and pastries.
RICK: I see. Maybe… any interesting story you could share? Something?
CELESTE: Really nothing to tell. Waking up, arriving at 9, staying there waiting for customers until 5. End of story.
RICK: And time travel?
CELESTE: I travel every day.
RICK: But you said you don’t travel as much.
CELESTE: I only arrive in serene places. I stop somewhere on an evening bridge in the 78th century and silently gaze into the distance. Something like that.
RICK: Honestly, I respect that.
CELESTE: Thank you. But what about you?
RICK: Ah. Eheh. You really want me to find a starter?
CELESTE: Is it that complicated with Charlotte as a company?
RICK: Not just Charlotte. We have a web of time travelers. I have my best friend, a sister figure, who accidentally discovered time travel. Only after that, I met Charlotte, and the chaos intensified. Hey, if there’s a chance to meet another time, I might tell something.
CELESTE: You seem to be enthusiastic about it.
RICK: Well, there are fun bits, admittedly.
CELESTE: Admittedly? Mixed experience?
RICK: Uh, er… That’s a territory where I prefer to keep the door closed.
CELESTE: I respect it. Sorry if I might be pushy.
RICK: Oh no-no, you’re not. Not at all. You’re perfectly fine. Just… it’s a me problem.
CELESTE: Difficult past?
RICK: (pause) Yes.
CELESTE: I understand.

CHARLOTTE returns with three ice creams.

CHARLOTTE: Alright, my friends. Looks like today is a quiet one. To be honest, I do need a few hours of break from my lifestyle. So, Cely, I apologize for my outburst.
CELESTE: Don’t apologize. What have you brought?
CHARLOTTE: Three ice creams. Different flavors.
CELESTE: I can see that.
RICK: She meant the flavor, Char.
CHARLOTTE: Rick, coffee and chocolate with soft biscuits and bits of strawberry, that’s yours.
RICK: Well remembered, thanks.
CHARLOTTE: Cele, vanilla with blueberry syrup and tiny bits of mint and lemon, that’s yours.
CELESTE: No way. You remember my favorite flavor!
CHARLOTTE: Don’t underestimate my memory, sunshine. I know how you were fangirling over it when you were in fourth grade.
RICK: Let me guess yours. Bits of apple and pineapple!
CHARLOTTE: Right first time!
CELESTE: That’s so un-Victorian.
CHARLOTTE: Hey! Do I look like an absolutist?
CELESTE: No. Your flavor combination is more like a crime against cuisine.
CHARLOTTE: Says the person who thinks mixing blueberries with lemon is not causing mental suffering to the average 21st-century citizen. Rick, she works in a pastry shop!
CELESTE: I don’t consider the 21st-century a pinnacle of ideal taste, to be fair. Uh, no offense, Rick, it’s my culture.
RICK: Oh don’t tell me. I know how wacky culture can be. I’ll never stop talking about that weird green fruit from 2345.
CHARLOTTE: Rick.
RICK: What?
CHARLOTTE: PTT is on _your_ forearm.
RICK: So?
CHARLOTTE: Remember I said I wanted to google that fruit? And I asked _you_ to remind me later.
RICK: You want me to google now? We’re eating.
CHARLOTTE: Blooming heck, what a dilettante. (grabs RICK’s arm, unfolds PTT, and talks to it) Hey. 2345, green fruit with very strong, distinctive flavor.
LISA (VO): You could ask me.
RICK: (to CHARLOTTE) And I could handle it without your help, Charlotte, thank you very much.
LISA (VO): Oh, Rick? That’s sensational. Why is this PTT on your hand?
RICK: That was our agreement.
CHARLOTTE: It really was.
LISA (VO): Ah. So it means that I can tease you more often now. More directly. More personally. How exciting! That’s fabulous!!
RICK: Yeah, yeah, great, now please focus. Can you help us find an information about the green fruit we found in 2345? Its specific characteristic is flavor, it’s very strong, so you have to consume it in very tiny amounts.
LISA (VO): Honestly, I would like to arrive personally and try it out. Maybe that would help to give you more precise output, measurements, and molecular structure. My physical model already contains operational taste sensors.
RICK: Focus!!
LISA (VO): Right, right. Flamgolia.
RICK: Excuse me?
LISA (VO): The name of the fruit is flamgolia.
RICK: Is this genuine information?
LISA (VO): Databanks of the year 2345 AD on planet Earth has 64 known legitimate, verified scientific and agricultural sources describing flamgolia. Flamgolia has a shape nearly identical to apple and lemon, the shape is somewhat merged. The color of the fruit is predominantly bright green, however red variant has been encountered as well. Flamgolia can glow at night, it’s fluorescent. Grows primarily in the southern regions of South America, as well as in Australia and New Zealand. Shall I dive deeper? For instance, if flamgolia is safe for consumption during pregnancy—
RICK: No, thank you, that was quite informative.
CHARLOTTE: And rather entertaining.
LISA (VO): If you want more of entertaining, just take me into your trips! Well, my physical counterpart! I begin to experience human death from boredom here in 2038 because Scarlett is overloading my processing power with tactical intelligence!!
RICK: We’ll think about it.
LISA (VO): And yet, one question. Are you two serious?
CHARLOTTE: Yes! Now shut up! (folds and switches off PTT)
CELESTE: What a strange name, flamgolia…
CHARLOTTE: Hey, you’re a time travel master, and this is notably strange to you? Really?
CELESTE: Charlotte. There’s a difference between your unhinged style of “being thrown around and hoping for the best” and systematic, academic, calculated approach.
CHARLOTTE: Rick, can you hear me yawning already? A notepad full of “yawn, yawn, yawn, yawn”? Ah yeah, I’m already past that point.
CELESTE: No, I’m saying, there’s a difference between theory and practice.
CHARLOTTE: So?
CELESTE: We had a lot of intense learning on every possible, perceivable kind of paradoxes. But unlike our school, which had an impossibly rare exception and shortly after had it revoked, university wasn’t granted the permission to use temporal displacement devices for educational purposes until the graduated person proves their ideal competence in an intensive series of exams.
CHARLOTTE: Girl, you literally have your own PTT on your hand! More than that, you have more competence for operating it than all of us combined! Go to some 1950s party or something!! Don’t pretend you don’t know what to do with this power, you _perfectly_ know!
CELESTE: You don’t understand. I don’t have your mentality to think of this. I’ve been taught to use it for strictly practical scenarios.
CHARLOTTE: Uh-huh. Transporting to the 236th century to observe the constellations, then returning to your 59th as if you went for a stroll. The epitome of practicality.
RICK: Char! Stop!
CHARLOTTE: Rick?
RICK: We’re having a rest. Not a lecture battle for the coolest vision of time travel.
CHARLOTTE: I’m sorry.
CELESTE: Charlotte. May I ask if you’re still interested in the literature? Don’t think I would ever forget your Victorian Club.
RICK: Ah. Peace offering. Charley, don’t downplay your five-book stack you took that day in 1838. You were going to read about French Revolution there, remember?
CHARLOTTE: As if I would ever downplay it. You said the dangerous word, Cely! Never say literature in my presence. Never.
RICK: Translation: she’s already in.
CHARLOTTE: Correct!
CELESTE: Library would really help to keep my mind in peace.
RICK: That’s a very nice bridge. Char?
CHARLOTTE: Absolutely let’s go! I’m gonna build a tower of books, and you Rick have to skim through one of them at the very least.
RICK: Quest accepted.
