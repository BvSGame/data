# Parent’s Play: Part 1

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **August 9, 2026** by **liledix⁴**. Edited in **August 10, 2026**.

----

46th-century Earth. The hallway with the ticket booth. CASHIER (young male human) asks simple questions and gives tickets. We see a long line of people. 7 of them are from Team Saylor.

SAYLOR: Char, can you kindly tell us where we are?
CHARLOTTE: It’s a little surprise! Just walk to that box office.
SAYLOR: Is this a show?
CHARLOTTE: Yes.
SAYLOR: Is it safe?
CHARLOTTE: I mean, I played it eight times already. I won five times. See any missing part of my body?
SAYLOR: I have a feeling that you could lose your brain here someday.
CHARLOTTE: Oh don’t worry, we’ll win. (to STELLE) Right, Stelle?
STELLE: Huh?
CHARLOTTE: Don’t mind us.

The line moves. MARIE opens the Team Saylor line.

CASHIER: Name.
MARIE: Uh… Marie.
CASHIER: Age.
MARIE: 21.
CASHIER: Parental status.
MARIE: Uh… Are you asking this to everyone?
CASHIER: Yes. Parental status.
MARIE: Um. I’m… not a parent. (looking at RICK) Yet.
RICK: Don’t even dream of it, devil in corporate outfit.
MARIE: Huh!
CASHIER: Your ticket.
MARIE: No need to pay?
CASHIER: No. Take your seat.
MARIE: Thanks. (to RICK) Can you lie about parental status? Just for the fun of it?
RICK: No.
MARIE: Vibe killer.

MARIE goes down the hallway. RICK is next.

SCARLETT: (whispering) Good job.
RICK: (nods)
CASHIER: Name.
RICK: Rick. Short for Richard.
CASHIER: Is Rick acceptable?
RICK: Yep.
CASHIER: Age.
RICK: 20.
CASHIER: Parental status.
RICK: I’m not a parent. I mean, I have university to finish first.
SAYLOR: (echoing) Tough!
CASHIER: Your ticket.
RICK: Thank you.

RICK turns to SCARLETT and talks to her half-voiced quickly, knowing that there’s a line behind them.

RICK: Uh, Scarlett? I suspect Marie is waiting for me there.
SCARLETT: No, she wouldn’t. Just stay here for a few seconds more so that she could actually sit down. Have you peeked her row and seat number over her shoulder, by the way?
RICK: Of course. I have some of your training expertise. Tactical stealth and stuff.
SCARLETT: You speak my language, my boy. So?
RICK: It’s 13-59. 13th row, 59th seat.
SCARLETT: And yours?
RICK: (glances at the ticket) Oh no…
SCARLETT: 60th seat…
RICK: Same row… Oh I’m doomed…
SCARLETT: No, you aren’t. See? Tickets are not randomized. I’ll be sitting right next to you.
RICK: In your presence, she’ll treat me as the war zone!
SCARLETT: Wait for me in the hallway. Don’t go yet. We’ll discuss our situation.

RICK nods. SCARLETT pats RICK’s shoulder and he goes. It’s SCARLETT’s turn.

CASHIER: Name.
SCARLETT: Scarlett. Double T, please.
CASHIER: Age.
SCARLETT: It’s classified.
CASHIER: Age.
LISA: Please, Scarlett, don’t argue. This system can’t be outmaneuvered.
SCARLETT: Sigh… 24.
CASHIER: Parental status.
SCARLETT: Not a parent. But considering an infantility of my colleagues, sometimes I feel myself like a surrogate mother of the group.
CASHIER: Your ticket.
SCARLETT: What are these tickets for, anyway?
CASHIER: They’re for the audience.
SCARLETT: Audience. So, there are also tickets _not_ for audience?
CASHIER: No. Take your seat.
SCARLETT: Hm. Alright.

SCARLETT walks to RICK who was waiting for her as she asked.

RICK: So?
SCARLETT: Just as I told you. 61st seat. It’s not random.
RICK: How can you be sure that I’m safer when you sit next?
SCARLETT: Oh, Rick. It’s worse without me. In this case, she would try to swallow you alive. Literally. Physically. Without the slightest shame or regret.
RICK: I remember her trick. There was a time in 2024 when she bought two tickets for a sketchy movie in the back rows.
SCARLETT: Please tell me you didn’t fall for it.
RICK: I was lucky. Saylor needed my urgent help. She had to go to the convenience store and I had to look after Stelle.
SCARLETT: How convenient.
RICK: Yeah, we had a fun time. Stelle was reading washing machine manual with such marvel in her eyes. Truly her peak of the 21st-century discoveries. It’s been more amusing than any movie Marie would ever suggest.
SCARLETT: You got lucky with your surrogate sister getting technically biological daughter, right?
RICK: The ultimate cheat code to dodge Marie’s shenanigans. You love to have it.
SCARLETT: I’m proud of you, really. I like to see your long years of deeply rooted trauma being healed so naturally.
RICK: Yeah, thanks. So. What’s your tactic?
SCARLETT: It’s basic. Every time she invades your personal space, and she certainly will, you watch me dehyping her romantic inclinations in a second.
RICK: Dehyping? Is this _your_ lingo?
SCARLETT: 2038 speak. Don’t pay attention.
RICK: I hope _you_ won’t invade my space just to counterattack her.
SCARLETT: Rick. I’m an agent, yes, but I don’t use Marie’s methods. Have some respect.
RICK: Well. Us walking in side-by-side will slightly unsettle her for sure.
SCARLETT: That’s exactly why I asked you to wait. I’m sure she planned something ahead. Let us be the first to launch the attack.
RICK: Fine. Let’s go see how her heart flips.
SCARLETT: Ha-ha.

Back to the line. It’s LISA’s turn.

LISA: Hi, peasant!
CASHIER: Name.
LISA: Lisa. Or Liz. Or Elizabeth. Or Please-set-an-alarm-for-8-AM.
CASHIER: Is Lisa acceptable?
LISA: Just call me Miss Fabulous.
CASHIER: This is not your name.
LISA: And if I lied? (STELLE pushed her in the back) Fine, fine. Record me as Lisa.
CASHIER: Age.
LISA: Well, heh, my physical body was maintained for 27 days.
CASHIER: Age.
LISA: What? Oh, I see. Outward appearance. (takes a mirror out of her pocket, takes a good look at herself and her body) Well, I think I look like an 18-year-old. And 3 months. That’s my subjective evaluation. It may vary.
CASHIER: Is 18 acceptable?
LISA: I have no intention of delaying the line. Yes! Yes, consider it a definitive statement. 18 years old. Whatever it means.
CASHIER: Parental status.
LISA: Um. I can’t be a parent. I’m an android. I don’t have reproductive functionality by nature.
CASHIER: Confirm you’re a robot.
LISA: I’m Human Plus! Fine, fine, I’m a synthetic being. I have chassis and uh… (demonstratively detaches her wrist) …detachable wrists.
CASHIER: Do you understand parenting?
LISA: You insult me! I’m a pro! I have a large dedicated databank titled Parenting. And child behind gets the best out of this package.
CASHIER: You’re a participant. Walk to the left.
LISA: Wait. Am I not an audience?
CASHIER: You’re a participant. Walk to the left.
LISA: Fine, fine. I remembered the first time.

LISA goes. Now STELLE.

CASHIER: Name.
STELLE: Stelle.
CASHIER: Age.
STELLE: 16.
CASHIER: Your ticket.
STELLE: Oh, that’s correct of you not to ask for parental status.
CASHIER: You’re underage.
STELLE: Yes, yes, we’ll not debate on this. Thank you.

STELLE goes. Now SAYLOR.

CASHIER: Name.
SAYLOR: Saylor. Saylor with a Y. That’s very important.
CASHIER: Age.
SAYLOR: 20. Well, since I was born. Some people claim that I’m over 11,000 years old, it’s a blatant rumor.
CASHIER: Parental status.
SAYLOR: I… (whispering to CHARLOTTE) Does she count? (CHARLOTTE nods) I’m a parent.
CASHIER: How many descendants do you have?
SAYLOR: That’s a bit invasive talk, isn’t it? One. I have one child.
CASHIER: You’re a participant. Walk to the left.
SAYLOR: Hm. Interesting. Let’s see what we have here, then.

SAYLOR goes. Now CHARLOTTE.

CASHIER: Name.
CHARLOTTE: Crazy Daisy. My mom gave me this name. Write it as one word.
CASHIER: Age.
CHARLOTTE: 22.
CASHIER: Parental status.
CHARLOTTE: Three kids. Don’t ask how. I have tricks.
CASHIER: You’re a participant.
CHARLOTTE: I know. I go left.

CHARLOTTE catches up to SAYLOR.

SAYLOR: Hey. Mind explaining now?
CHARLOTTE: This is a game show. Essentially, this is a quiz for parents.
SAYLOR: Lisa is not a parent.
CHARLOTTE: She participates because the rules of the game state explicitly that the robots with the parental expertise are also allowed to participate.
SAYLOR: And what are the questions? The acceptable temperature of milk for toddlers?
CHARLOTTE: That too.
SAYLOR: Is it fun?
CHARLOTTE: It’s friendly rivalry of mommies. Wanna try your skills?
SAYLOR: Let’s see who is more parent-y.
CHARLOTTE: Let’s do it.

To be continued in Part 2.
