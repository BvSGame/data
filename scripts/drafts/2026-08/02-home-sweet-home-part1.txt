# Home Sweet Home: Part 1

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **August 8, 2026** by **liledix⁴**. Edited in **August 10, 2026**.

----

2024, SAYLOR’s rental house.

SAYLOR is sitting at the kitchen table, focused, doing her homework.

STELLE: Mom! Thank goodness you’ve made a separate room for entertainment! I watched some movies, and my brain went wobble-wobble-wobble because the story had so many twists I had to rewatch it three times to understand what’s going on.
SAYLOR: Uh-huh…
STELLE: (sits down at the table) Mom? Something wrong?
SAYLOR: Nah, darling. Just personal crisis. (turns to STELLE) Look at you. You’re walking my exact path. You watch movies too. (back to the homework) Except I almost never watched voluntarily, Jenna forced me.

SAYLOR keeps writing as she talks. Focused. Laser-focused.

STELLE: I remember that stepmom story. Movie night stuff. She cherished your presence. That was her way of bonding.
SAYLOR: Yeah… But you… I don’t even ask you. I never would. You watch everything, read everything, play everything, on your own. You have an enthusiasm so unhealthy that you make me want to watch an ep of some show too.
STELLE: It’s exciting! 2024 media is exciting. Honestly? I don’t even want to leave this period. You’re so lucky to have so much interesting stuff. I won’t have enough of my lifespan to consume everything.
SAYLOR: That’s what worries me. Mass media is a dangerous thing. Open this can of worms, and it will consume you.
STELLE: I hate the concept of soap operas, actually.
SAYLOR: Thank goodness I don’t allow you to have social media. Because in this case, I’m afraid you’ll be stuck in this house forever, voluntarily. Until I manage to cut internet cable or cancel the contract with the cell phone provider.
STELLE: I’m aware of the dragons, mom. You don’t have to question my willpower. I use internet only for browsing knowledge. Wikipedia is an incredibly enjoyable place, you know. It’s very interesting to look at how the humanity sees the world through a 21st-century lens. I don’t understand the purpose of commenting online, though.
SAYLOR: You have me. You have Rick. You have Charlotte. You have Scarlett. You have many other people, living and breathing. Why would you also need to talk to people you’ll never see in person?
STELLE: That’s exactly my argument. But you do.
SAYLOR: I talk to those people because I and Rick have an online business. We record videos where we assemble some small robots or custom gadgets, and that’s how we have some financial support.
STELLE: I see. You have a community to care about.
SAYLOR: Yep. Well, Charlotte also helps finding freelance work.
STELLE: Cherry?!
SAYLOR: Yeah. She’s hyperactive, she’s unable to sit still, it’s against her nature. But she can’t keep up with everything. That’s where I come in. She says: “Hey sunshine, we have a task, and I promise you’ll be paid fairly. I give you the word of a time traveler.” Essentially she’s my agent, ha-ha. She really knows where to seek a coin, she’s pretty talented.
STELLE: I never knew this side of her. Amazing.
SAYLOR: Maybe she’ll find some work for you as well.
STELLE: (eyes shining) For me?
SAYLOR: It’s usually pretty simple. You can fix formatting and typos in the documents. You can do proper calculations for reports. You can do content management for some websites.
STELLE: Boring…
SAYLOR: Hey, it’s still a job and you don’t do it for free! Charley built enough reputation to state firmly: “Look, my name Char doesn’t stand for ‘charity.’ Pay for the work, and we’ll do it. If you don’t wanna, blooming heck, find someone else.” And you know you need any bit of money for basic survival.
STELLE: I know, mom, I know. But uh, why are you so sad today?
SAYLOR: Actually, by the middle of this conversation, I had stopped thinking about the unpleasant stuff.
STELLE: Unpleasant stuff? Mind sharing it?
SAYLOR: Okay. It’s uh, mostly about education. I still have to study and I’m afraid I don’t keep up. Don’t mind my focus right now.
STELLE: Ah, right. You’re still a student.
SAYLOR: Well, naturally. I’m 20, what did you expect?
STELLE: But you don’t study in this town.
SAYLOR: No, I study remotely. Instead of watching movies, I watch recorded lessons, or live ones if I’m lucky.
STELLE: I want to watch some too. Just for the fun of it.
SAYLOR: Why not.
STELLE: But why are you visiting the local university, anyway?
SAYLOR: Ah. Some weeks ago, I was invited to this town.
STELLE: Invited.
SAYLOR: Yeah. The most talented tech students were requested.
STELLE: A group. Like a special reinforcement, a backup.
SAYLOR: We were asked to do what the local tech university couldn’t figure out.
STELLE: The glitch of the sleepwalkers?
SAYLOR: Yes. We were invited here to analyze their behavior. In this town, this is the first time I’ve met those tinheads. There were only a few of them. Serving as the assistants for basic jobs, to ease the workflow.
STELLE: But?
SAYLOR: Every night, when those androids were supposed to hibernate and auto-recharge, approximately a three-minute glitch happened at random times. During that glitch, they were waking up and walking aimlessly. Once the glitch ended, they got back to hibernating.
STELLE: So that’s why they’re called sleepwalkers…
SAYLOR: Mm-hm.
STELLE: Why bomb them if they were just… walking?
SAYLOR: Self-defense. They targeted me when I arrived.
STELLE: You? What’s so special about you?
SAYLOR: I’m still figuring out. Probably because they were on a mission all along. Probably they want to steal the fridge.
STELLE: The vintage one. The one that shares the electric power. Why would they need it?
SAYLOR: I don’t know. But I suspect they targeted me because it was me who unlocked the door to this house that already had that fridge and the time machine SUV.
STELLE: I have a feeling that they’re related to time travel. For a reason.
SAYLOR: How else would you explain their appearance in many other time periods? Where they’re _still_ targeting me?
STELLE: Either your fridge or time machine has something worth deploying thousands of robots for.
SAYLOR: That’s most likely. Whatever it is, it clearly doesn’t belong to them.
STELLE: Those don’t belong to you either.
SAYLOR: I borrowed them.
STELLE: Uh-huh, uh-huh.
SAYLOR: If an owner “forgot” this stuff here, it’s not my problem anymore. If they really appear here and ask to take that back for themselves, fine. So far, it didn’t happen. If it’s a part of the furniture, I use it. Legally.
STELLE: Have you asked an owner about this?
SAYLOR: It wasn’t me who was talking. It was Jenna. That person was pretty strange and… they have some mysterious aura, you know. Like they don’t say enough…
STELLE: Hmm…
SAYLOR: But, oh well, this is the cheapest house. Honestly? Can’t complain. Two floors. Four bedrooms. Nice kitchen. Lovely living room. Good garage. Big basement. Problems? Annoying robotic neighbors. Anything else? No.
STELLE: Lack of backyard.
SAYLOR: Come on. Why would you need it for? Pool parties?
STELLE: I wouldn’t say no, honestly.
SAYLOR: Swimming lessons. Not far away from here.
STELLE: BBQ.
SAYLOR: Front yard. I don’t care about opinions.
STELLE: Gardening.
SAYLOR: Banned.
STELLE: But why?
SAYLOR: We’re not wasting time on flower care! Do it in your own room, if you want. Windowsills and flower pots exist for a reason.
STELLE: Basement?
SAYLOR: It serves strategic purpose.
STELLE: Such as?
SAYLOR: (puts the pen down, looks at STELLE) Now? Really? Stelle, I’m doing my homework!
STELLE: If you show me the basement, I’ll help you. And you know I’m your living cheat sheet.
SAYLOR: Fine… (gets up) Come along.
STELLE: 16 years of concentrated pod education. Must be low-key illegal to have this power for your third year. A+ only for every discipline.
SAYLOR: Terrifying. But I’ll manage.
