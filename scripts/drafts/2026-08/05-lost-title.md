# `<LOST TITLE>`

A script draft for _Bombs vs. Sleepwalkers_. Written in **August 18–19, 2026** by **liledix⁴**.

----

2024, SAYLOR’s rental house. Living room. STELLE, wearing green tank top, sits at the table, frantically doing something on her brand new laptop. SAYLOR enters, wearing denim overall dress as usual.

SAYLOR: Hey! How are you doing?
STELLE: (trying to hide her quiet panic) Uh… I’m fine, thanks.
SAYLOR: Anything good with the new toy, my week-old teenage pie?
STELLE: It’s pretty solid for a budget model. But most importantly, it’s so lightweight, thin and compact. How does it manage to hold battery life for 2 hours?
SAYLOR: My savings are having some pretty significant dent, thanks to your growing needs. I bought you a phone, now you have it like a civilized member of society.
STELLE: I still don’t understand its real practicality, it’s incredibly limited feature-wise.
SAYLOR: As a tablet user myself, relatable. And now you have your own 13-inch laptop.
STELLE: Mom, this is an investment. My developments would cover our expenses eventually. Once I figure out how it works, that is.
SAYLOR: I'm not questioning it. I know you're clever enough to come up with solutions.
STELLE: Speaking of which… There’s one thing I’m stuck with…
SAYLOR: What is it?
STELLE: I’ve uh… lost my files permanently from the hard drive… The beautiful hierarchy I’ve built… Gone.
SAYLOR: What?
STELLE: The program I interacted with is safe. I know what viruses are, so it’s not that. But I messed up the configuration and it wiped out my files. Really, software of your era is so unnecessarily destructive. There _is_ a way to recover them, right? It’s not that simple to destroy files, is it? Like, they _must_ stay for a while.
SAYLOR: Turn your laptop off.
STELLE: How would it help?
SAYLOR: (sits down at the table next to STELLE) Turn it off! Right now!! The forced way! Don’t save any changes, you don’t need to overwrite anything.
STELLE: Right. (holds the power button) So I was correct. The residual content. It’s only marked “can be destroyed,” not _outright_ destroyed.
SAYLOR: Exactly. (pulls out a flash drive from the pocket of her denim dress) Live USB system. Plug this flash drive in. (points at the top row of the keyboard) Press this key repeatedly, then load a second device from the list. Or wherever you see words USB or live on the screen.
STELLE: What would it do?
SAYLOR: Load the system from the flash drive, avoiding any writing operations on the hard drive where you lost your files. If you use a recovery software, there is a chance to get your files back. But uh, don’t feel safe losing them every time you experiment with something. Try virtual machines next time, okay?
STELLE: You have to teach me.
SAYLOR: I teased it intentionally. You have so much to learn, my child. But for now, how long ago did you lose your stuff?
STELLE: I played with configurations only three minutes ago.
SAYLOR: There is still some chance.
CHARLOTTE: (quietly sneaks in from behind) Boo?
STELLE: (unfrightened) Cherry, get out, we’re busy.
CHARLOTTE: Yeah, just ignore my stealth mode tests.
SAYLOR: She means it.
CHARLOTTE: Why so serious? What’s the matter?
SAYLOR: She’s learning how to recover deleted files.
CHARLOTTE: Ah… Child’s play. (to STELLE) Consider cloud storage later on. I have some interesting suggestions.
SAYLOR: Cloud storage?! Charley, it means being at the mercy of a damn remote computer, do you realize that? That’s literally zero op-sec!
CHARLOTTE: Calm the heck down. I’m only suggesting my infrastructure that has end-to-end encryption for every single file.
SAYLOR: Here she goes… We need an ad blocker in this house… “Do you know who doesn’t need recovery routines? Our sponsor!”
CHARLOTTE: (continues) I would never know what my servers have, and they’re secure enough that I know only my most trusted friends have access to them.
STELLE: And what is the storage capacity?
CHARLOTTE: It’s pretty cozy but I keep expanding it.
STELLE: How big is it?
CHARLOTTE: 120 petabytes. Any good?
STELLE: 120 petabytes?!
CHARLOTTE: (scratches the back of her head) Well. I don’t count shadow drives for avoiding file corruption.
STELLE: Wait, but… electric bills.
CHARLOTTE: Come on, kid. I paid for a year in advance. I know the average consumption and it isn’t that much. It’s pretty optimized. (unfolds her PTT to reveal touchscreen display) Now there must be a meme I saved yesterday in my cloud…
SAYLOR: Your… time transporter…
CHARLOTTE: Nothing special, just a screen. Uh… Sayley, why are you staring like that?
SAYLOR: It’s… your PTT has a different layout. I don’t recognize the buttons.
CHARLOTTE: Ah. I took a different model. What do you think?
SAYLOR: Still screams “I wear an anachronistic pager on my wrist but it travels in time. And it needs an electric umbrella for some reason.”
CHARLOTTE: You just don’t fancy the portability.
SAYLOR: I don’t want my inner organs to be portable during time experiments, yes.
CHARLOTTE: So you choose to travel in a box with wheels that is an old SUV, like canned beans.
STELLE: Uh, guys?
SAYLOR & CHARLOTTE: Yes?
STELLE: It worked. The files are back.
CHARLOTTE: Now secure them on my cloud. I’ll send you a link to download client, and the server parameters. (whispering to SAYLOR) Saylor? May I have you for a minute?
SAYLOR: Sure. (to STELLE) Uh, is everything fine now?
STELLE: Yeah, it must be. Don’t mind me, mom.
SAYLOR: (pats STELLE’s head) Alright.

CHARLOTTE asks SAYLOR in whisper.

CHARLOTTE: She’s so sweet, can I adopt her?
SAYLOR: (crosses arms) We discussed this. Only one mother exists for Stelle ever. Me. She herself actively justifies it: she has my part of DNA.
CHARLOTTE: Hey. I can be a second mother.
SAYLOR: (hesitantly suspicious) What are you implying?
CHARLOTTE: Admit it. Parenting the child alone is fairly tricky. Every child needs two pairs of hands.
SAYLOR: Hello? Three kids! Remember?
CHARLOTTE: Two of them grew up, another one has a nanny. Come on, throw logistics out the window, it’s gonna be fun.
SAYLOR: I don’t like your ambiguous subtext.
CHARLOTTE: What subtext?
SAYLOR: I almost suspect you want to take a role of a second parent because of… me.
CHARLOTTE: (facepalming) No. No-no-no. Now you’re making it weird.
SAYLOR: Am I?
CHARLOTTE: (lowers her head, eyes down) I didn’t mean that at all! Sigh…

CHARLOTTE gently puts hands on SAYLOR’s shoulders. SAYLOR’s arms remain lowered. They stay like that for minutes as they talk.

CHARLOTTE: I mean, you’re stellar and all, as a person, but. Really. Get a grip.
SAYLOR: It’s just suspicious.
CHARLOTTE: (worried, looking eye to eye) You seriously need a vacation, a long one. You’re traveling too much, it affects your reasoning. (looks at STELLE with fondness, voice slightly cracking) Just look at Stelle. What a gorgeous prodigy. She’s ultimately everything I ever wanted to be. Why couldn’t she take some of my experience? Let’s be reasonable, Saylie.
SAYLOR: So you’re framing it as a pragmatic approach.
CHARLOTTE: (looking at SAYLOR again) Like a proper parenting should be.
SAYLOR: Sigh… It’s tempting. We may try, maybe. I don’t know. But even if I agree, how would we explain this to her?
CHARLOTTE: Uh… Yeah, that’s… heh, tricky, isn’t it?
SAYLOR: What if I try to talk to her delicately?
CHARLOTTE: Like what?
SAYLOR: You know… “Hey, sweetie, imagine if Charley was your mother. How would you feel about that?”
CHARLOTTE: I don’t know, it’s too on the nose.
SAYLOR: Well, it’s the best I have!
CHARLOTTE: Nah, you could do better. Try this: “Hey, look. You already treat Charlotte as an aunt, and fittingly so, but if she spent more time with you, do you think the dynamic might change?”
SAYLOR: Hm. It may work.
CHARLOTTE: It might.
SAYLOR: Well, about you. Are you staying or what?
CHARLOTTE: Yeah. Yeah, I have nothing else to do today, might as well spend some time with you lot.
SAYLOR: Make yourself comfortable. Hah, second mother indeed.
CHARLOTTE: Are we playing Monopoly today?
SAYLOR: Surely will. Surely will. Wanna see who of the mothers beats the game of buying and selling?
CHARLOTTE: Let’s be real, Stelle will destroy us.
SAYLOR: Ha-ha, she can. (whispering) Don’t try poker with her, she’s a menace.
CHARLOTTE: (stepping closer) Ahem… You already treat me as the best friend, and fittingly so, but if I spent more time with you, do you think the dynamic might change?
SAYLOR: Damn…

SAYLOR bursts out laughing, CHARLOTTE laughs as well.

CHARLOTTE: That was a joke.
SAYLOR: Well, if I answered seriously, it might.
CHARLOTTE: Oh wow… Really? Interesting. And how would you see it?
SAYLOR: Best best friend. (lets go of CHARLOTTE’s hands from her shoulders) Now don’t push your luck.
CHARLOTTE: Oh, Saylor. (pulls out her previous PTT from her pocket) Too bad I’ve already planned it out.
SAYLOR: Your “old” PTT. The one you used most of the time, pun not intended.
CHARLOTTE: (puts PTT into SAYLOR’s hands) It’s yours now. Take it.
SAYLOR: Why?
CHARLOTTE: (getting slightly emotional) Newer model satisfies me. It works with the stealth mode better. Besides, I trust your engineering skills, so mod it however you want.
SAYLOR: Thanks, I guess. I’ll take it as a valuable souvenir for now, but we’ll see.
CHARLOTTE: (almost begins to cry) Thank you.

CHARLOTTE hugs tightly and squeals so softly you can barely hear her.

CHARLOTTE: (softly crying) Thank you for living and breathing.
SAYLOR: Hey-hey. It’s alright. It’s alright. Come on, Char, pull yourself together.
CHARLOTTE: Spoken like a mom. (stops hugging, but keeps her hands on SAYLOR’s shoulders) Cup of tea?
SAYLOR: What else do you even drink, Miss Victorian Storm?
CHARLOTTE: But Earl Grey this time. Not your odd bubblegum disaster.
SAYLOR: Fine, fine. Go fetch Monopoly.
CHARLOTTE: On it.

## P.S.

We see a massive building with the rows upon rows of racks. Looks like a server building.

Flash! Bang! CHARLOTTE and STELLE appear.

STELLE: Where are we?
CHARLOTTE: My server. The exact cloud storage I was talking about.
STELLE: So huge…
CHARLOTTE: Naturally. The whole infrastructure with 120 petabytes of the main storage and 3 arrays of the shadow storage takes a lot of physical space.
STELLE: What time are we in exactly?
CHARLOTTE: 2079.
STELLE: But how do you access 2079 servers from 2024 or other time periods?
CHARLOTTE: A small relay server. We have to visit it later.
STELLE: It’s pretty cold in here. Ooh, quite freezing.
CHARLOTTE: Disk activity radiates heat. You have to have a giant refrigerator to counterbalance it.
STELLE: I see. So. I see rows upon rows of hard drive racks.
CHARLOTTE: A single 8-foot tall rack contains 100 hard drives. Each hard drive has 1 terabyte of space.
STELLE: 100 terabytes per rack.
CHARLOTTE: Yes.
STELLE: How many racks does a single row contain?
CHARLOTTE: 50.
STELLE: 5 petabytes… As we walk through these racks, one row to the left and one row to the right, we immediately walk through 10 million gigabytes…
CHARLOTTE: Of the end-to-end encrypted data.
STELLE: Stellar…
CHARLOTTE: That’s Saylor’s word, do you know that?
STELLE: I know. Takes time to come up with my own catchphrase. Cut me some slack, I’m only one week old.
CHARLOTTE: You know, I just enjoy walking around here. My inner nerd child is screaming.
STELLE: So. How many rows are here?
CHARLOTTE: About 100.
STELLE: Do you have a team that maintains these rows?
CHARLOTTE: Usually I manage myself. There is a central computer that tracks the statistics of each single drive. If some drive develops a fault, I just go and swap it for a new one.
STELLE: I have so many questions…
CHARLOTTE: Ha-ha, no wonder. Wanna join me every time I go here?
STELLE: I would be happy. You know… For an aunt, you’re very maternal. Do you know that?
CHARLOTTE: Well. Probably.
STELLE: Probably?
CHARLOTTE: I travel in time so often that I usually don’t have time to reflect on myself.
STELLE: Honestly, seeing how well you get along with my mom, I wouldn’t mind if you ever were my mother. Hypothetically. Even though it’s DNA that makes the rules.
CHARLOTTE: You’ve got to be kidding me.
STELLE: I mean, you’re good at it.
CHARLOTTE: You really think so?
STELLE: Look at me. I almost never say what I don’t think.
CHARLOTTE: It… takes some time for me to evaluate…
STELLE: Anyway. Any diagnostic to run here?
CHARLOTTE: Nope. I’ve last checked the stats 3 hours ago. It’s been pristine.
STELLE: I hope it stays that way for a long while.
CHARLOTTE: Let’s hope so.

6 hours later in their personal time. They’re back in 2024. SAYLOR’s rental house. SAYLOR walks down the stairs and meets CHARLOTTE in the living room.

CHARLOTTE: (quietly) Is she sleeping?
SAYLOR: Yes. She really gets tired easily. The slightest bit of fatigue knocks her out on the spot.
CHARLOTTE: It might be dangerous.
SAYLOR: That’s something I, or we, have to deal with.
CHARLOTTE: Tea time. Urgently.

Five minutes later. Kitchen. SAYLOR and CHARLOTTE are at the table. SAYLOR pours tea into mugs.

CHARLOTTE: Ah. Hibiscus.
SAYLOR: Something for a change.
CHARLOTTE: That’s nice.
SAYLOR: Tell me how your guiding trip was going.
CHARLOTTE: You would never believe me.
SAYLOR: Try me.
CHARLOTTE: Stelle said I’m good as a mother figure.
SAYLOR: Prompted by you.
CHARLOTTE: That’s the funniest bit, it wasn’t prompted! We literally talked about the structure of the infrastructure. Racks, rows, thermodynamics, all that stuff.
SAYLOR: She couldn’t.
CHARLOTTE: She just said! I quote: “Seeing how well you get along with my mom, I wouldn’t mind if you ever were my mother. Hypothetically. Even though it’s DNA that makes the rules.”
SAYLOR: Interesting.
CHARLOTTE: I felt uneasy since then.
SAYLOR: You brought it onto yourself, let’s be fair.
CHARLOTTE: Do you blame me?
SAYLOR: I don’t. I really don’t. Why have you even started thinking about it? The whole what-if thing?
CHARLOTTE: I mean, look at what we have… I already had an experience of having three children. Generated just like Stelle, but I wouldn’t do that if I didn’t feel the need for it.
SAYLOR: Need for what?
CHARLOTTE: Having maternal playground! Feeling like I could be responsible for someone. And you went the same route…
SAYLOR: Unwillingly!
CHARLOTTE: And your child turned out to be incredibly clever for her age.
SAYLOR: And she accepted you as an aunt figure…
CHARLOTTE: Quite quickly. She saw the outside world just for 3 days and I’m already an aunt of the prodigy. And you know how we spent the following days.
SAYLOR: I begin to genuinely see why she would find something in you. You never sit still. That’s an ideal role model for a teenage mind.
CHARLOTTE: And today she dropped that bomb.
SAYLOR: Okay. So we dodged the tricky talk. What’s next?
CHARLOTTE: Look. I’ll just keep hanging around. I don’t want to pressure. Let her make her own choices.
SAYLOR: Fair enough. We’ll just observe.
CHARLOTTE: Yes, forgetting the whole mom talk for now.
SAYLOR: We’ll see what Stelle herself would say. Just keep being your natural self.
